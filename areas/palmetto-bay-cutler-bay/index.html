What's different about floors here

  • Two housing stocks side by side. Palmetto Bay has many older ranch and mid-century homes on larger lots, especially toward Old Cutler Road, which may hide terrazzo. Cutler Bay is mostly 1990s and 2000s block subdivisions with tile over slab, two-car garages and backyard pools.
  • Hurricane Andrew's legacy. Andrew hit this part of the county hard in 1992. Many homes were rebuilt or built new afterwards, so many slabs are relatively modern and polish or coat well.
  • Low, flat and close to the bay. Neighborhoods east of Old Cutler Road and near the bay sit in higher-risk flood zones. Summer storms leave standing water, and slab moisture is the main reason garage coatings peel here.
  • Pools everywhere, many screened. Screens keep leaves out but hold shade and humidity, so decks grow mildew and stay damp. Open decks get more sun, heat and UV.
  • Old Cutler's canopy. Big trees bring roots, leaf stains and shade to driveways, walkways and decks.

Garage floors and interiors

Two-car garages are standard in both towns. The most reliable system is usually a full-flake floor with a polyaspartic topcoat that resists hot tires and UV. Test for moisture first, grind instead of etching, and remember a coating cleans up after water gets in under the door but doesn't flood-proof the garage. Garage floors.

  • Cutler Bay homes: tile over relatively modern slabs is a good candidate for a salt-and-pepper polish. Ask for a test patch. Residential polished concrete.
  • Older Palmetto Bay homes: check for terrazzo under tile or carpet. Restoration may beat replacement. Test old adhesives for asbestos.
  • Flood-prone homes: polished concrete and restored terrazzo clean up after water intrusion, where wood and laminate get replaced.
  • Driveways and walkways: resurface stained or worn concrete after fixing lifted sections and roots. HOAs may require color approval.
  • Shops and offices: polished concrete along the US-1 corridor, urethane cement in restaurant kitchens, seamless resin floors for clinics, scheduled after hours.

Approvals in Palmetto Bay and Cutler Bay

  • Palmetto Bay (a village) and Cutler Bay (a town) are separate municipalities, each with its own building department. Nearby unincorporated areas such as Perrine and South Miami Heights fall under Miami-Dade County.
  • HOAs in many subdivisions require approval for visible exterior changes like driveways, walkways and pool deck colors.
  • Interior polishing, coatings and resurfacing of existing slabs generally don't need a building permit, but structural repairs, screen-enclosure work and right-of-way driveway work may.

Common questions

What's the best pool deck finish in Palmetto Bay or Cutler Bay?

For most decks, a light-colored spray knockdown or cool deck finish. It's textured, slip resistant and stays cooler. Screened decks also suit flake or quartz systems with a textured, UV-stable topcoat.

Why does my screened pool deck get so much mildew?

Screens hold shade and humidity, so the deck stays damp. Clean and treat it, then use a breathable, mildew-resistant sealer.

Why do garage coatings peel here?

Mostly moisture from the shallow water table, plus poor prep and hot tires. Moisture testing and grinding prevent most failures.

Can I polish the slab under my tile?

In most Cutler Bay and newer Palmetto Bay homes, yes. Older Palmetto Bay homes may have terrazzo underneath, which may be worth restoring instead.

Do I need HOA approval?

Often for visible exterior changes like driveways and pool deck colors. Usually not for interior floors.

Do I need a permit?

Resurfacing and interior coatings usually don't. Structural work, screen enclosures and right-of-way driveway work may. Permits go through Palmetto Bay, Cutler Bay or Miami-Dade County depending on your address.

How long will a resurfaced pool deck last?

Typically 10 to 15 years or more, with resealing every 2 to 3 years.
