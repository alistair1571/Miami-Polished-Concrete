North Miami-Dade

Aventura Miami Shores

Typical buildings: high-rise condo towers in Aventura and Sunny Isles Beach; 1950s to 1970s homes in North Miami, North Miami Beach and Miami Shores; light industrial and hangars around Opa-locka.

Common floor questions

  • Condo association approval and sound-rated underlayment
  • Original terrazzo under carpet in mid-century homes
  • Hangar and fleet floors that need chemical-resistant coatings

Most requested: Microcement, Terrazzo restoration, Residential polished concrete, Epoxy flooring.

Miami Beach and the islands

Miami Beach Key Biscayne & Surfside

Typical buildings: Art Deco and MiMo buildings, oceanfront condos, hotels and restaurants in Miami Beach, Surfside, Bal Harbour, Bay Harbor Islands, North Bay Village and Key Biscayne.

Common floor questions

  • Hidden terrazzo in older buildings
  • Ground-floor moisture, sand and salt air
  • Historic district and condo approvals
  • Hotel and restaurant work phased around guests

Most requested: Terrazzo restoration, Commercial polished concrete, Epoxy flooring, Pool deck resurfacing.

Central Miami and the urban core

Brickell Downtown & Edgewater Wynwood Coconut Grove

Typical buildings: high-rise condos and offices, converted warehouses, galleries, restaurants and retail in Downtown, Edgewater, Midtown and the Design District, and older homes in Coconut Grove and Little Havana.

Common floor questions

  • High-rise rules: approval, post-tensioned slabs, elevator bookings and working hours
  • Restaurant and bar floors that get hosed down nightly
  • Gallery and retail floors that need to look perfect
  • Overnight work in occupied buildings

Most requested: Commercial polished concrete, Microcement, Epoxy flooring, Concrete staining.

West Miami-Dade and the industrial corridor

Doral Hialeah Medley Miami Lakes

Typical buildings: warehouses, distribution and logistics near MIA and the Palmetto; showrooms; manufacturing; townhomes and single-family homes in HOA communities in Hialeah Gardens, Miami Springs, Sweetwater and Westchester.

Common floor questions

  • Dusting warehouse slabs and joint damage from forklifts
  • Racking layouts and night or weekend work
  • Commercial kitchens and food production
  • HOA approval for driveways and exterior work

Most requested: Commercial polished concrete, Epoxy flooring, Garage floors, Concrete resurfacing.

South Miami-Dade

Coral Gables Pinecrest Kendall Palmetto Bay & Cutler Bay Homestead

Typical buildings: Mediterranean Revival and mid-century homes in Coral Gables; large family homes in Pinecrest and Palmetto Bay; 1970s to 90s block homes with tile in Kendall; newer subdivisions, farms and light industrial buildings in Homestead and the Redland.

Common floor questions

  • Tile tear-outs and what the slab looks like underneath
  • Coral Gables design review for visible exterior work
  • Pool decks and patios in the heat
  • Rust stains from irrigation wells on driveways and walkways

Most requested: Residential polished concrete, Pool deck resurfacing, Concrete resurfacing, Garage floors.

Local rules that can affect your floor

SituationWhat to know
Condo or co-opMost associations require written approval before hard-floor work, and many require sound-rated underlayment. Buildings may restrict working hours and elevator use.
HOA communitiesDriveways, walkways and other visible exterior work may need color and design approval.
Coral GablesVisible exterior changes may need design review. Check with the city before choosing outdoor finishes.
Miami Beach historic districtsExterior work on historic properties may need historic preservation review.
Driveway apronsWork in the public right-of-way may need a permit from your city or Miami-Dade County.
Older buildingsPre-mid-1980s floor tiles and adhesives may contain asbestos. See the asbestos guide.
City vs countyPermits, where needed, go through your city if you're in one of Miami-Dade's municipalities, or through Miami-Dade County in unincorporated areas such as much of Kendall.

Most interior polishing and coating work on an existing slab doesn't need a building permit, but your contractor should confirm for your address.

Why a local Miami-Dade contractor matters

  • Slabs and moisture: Miami's high water table and wet season affect every slab-on-grade floor and every coating. Local contractors test for it as standard.
  • Heat, UV and salt: outdoor finishes, sealers and garage coatings need products proven in South Florida conditions.
  • Buildings: from Art Deco terrazzo to post-tensioned high-rises, local experience changes what's safe and possible.
  • Rules: condo associations, HOAs, historic districts and city reviews.
  • Scheduling: the dry season, afternoon storms and hurricane-season planning.

Common questions

Do you cover all of Miami-Dade County?

Yes, from Aventura and Sunny Isles in the north to Homestead and Florida City in the south, including the beaches and Key Biscayne.

Do you cover Broward or Palm Beach?

Not currently. This site covers Miami-Dade County only.

Do I need a permit for polished concrete in Miami-Dade?

Polishing or coating an existing interior slab generally doesn't need a building permit, but condo associations, HOAs and some cities have their own approvals. Your contractor should confirm for your address.

Do you work in high-rise condos?

Yes. Expect association approval, sound rules, working-hour limits and elevator booking. Upper floors often suit microcement better than polishing.

Is there an extra charge for areas further away?

Any travel or minimum charge is stated in your written quote before you agree to anything.

How quickly can someone visit?

Usually within a few business days for a site visit anywhere in the county.
