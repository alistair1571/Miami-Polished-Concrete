Condo units: the sound rules come first

Some flooring companies will offer to polish or strip the floor in your Aventura unit without mentioning the rules. Before you spend anything, check them:

  • The code. Florida's building code requires floor/ceiling assemblies between dwelling units to reach IIC 50 for impact noise and STC 50 for airborne noise.
  • Your association may set a higher bar and require documented, tested assemblies for any hard floor.
  • A bare slab, polished or not, rates far below IIC 50, and there's no way to put an acoustic mat under a polished floor.
  • Ground-floor units with no neighbor below aren't covered by the between-units rule, though association rules still apply.

For upper floors: the concrete look is usually achieved with microcement over a tested, sound-rated build-up. Get approval in writing before removing any flooring. Full sound-rules guide

Terrazzo under the carpet

Many Aventura towers from the 1970s through the 1990s were finished with terrazzo, later covered by carpet or tile. It's often still in good condition, and restoring it can be far better value than new flooring. But there's a catch in condos:

  • Terrazzo is a hard floor. In a unit above another, exposing it may conflict with association rules that require carpet or a sound-rated floor.
  • Check the rules and get written approval before pulling up carpet.
  • If it's allowed, restoration involves filling tack-strip holes, grinding, honing and sealing. Terrazzo restoration
  • Old vinyl tile over terrazzo may sit on black adhesive. Test it for asbestos first. Asbestos guide

Lobbies, corridors and amenity spaces

For associations, common areas are where polished concrete (or restored terrazzo) makes most sense in a tower.

  • Lobbies: semi-gloss salt-and-pepper, with satin and walk-off matting at entrances where rain comes in.
  • Corridors: check what's directly below. Over parking, service or storage areas, polishing is usually straightforward; over units, sound rules apply.
  • Gyms, party rooms and pool-level spaces: often over non-residential areas; satin and anti-slip near pool doors.
  • Overnight phasing so residents always have access, plus a written maintenance spec for building staff. Maintenance
  • Paperwork: COI naming the association, licence, method statement, product data sheets, elevator booking and working hours.

Milestone inspections and what they mean for floors

Since the 2021 Surfside collapse, Florida requires condo buildings three or more stories tall to have a structural milestone inspection by a licensed engineer or architect, at 25 years for buildings within three miles of the coast, then every 10 years. Associations also need Structural Integrity Reserve Studies. In Aventura, that has meant major repair programs and, in some buildings, large special assessments.

  • Structural repairs come first. Floor finishes are cosmetic and aren't part of the structural work, whatever anyone selling floors says.
  • After repairs to corridors, lobbies, balconies or garages is usually the best time to choose new finishes, because those areas are already disrupted and surfaces are open.
  • Coordinate with the engineer on anything that touches repaired concrete, and on grind depth in post-tensioned slabs.
  • Budget realistically. Associations under assessment pressure often phase cosmetic work; a polished lobby or restored terrazzo can be a lower-cost upgrade than new stone or tile.

Parking garages and storage

  • Elevated garage decks in towers usually need waterproofing traffic-membrane systems specified by the building's engineer. That's specialist work, often part of repair programs.
  • Ground-level garage slabs, storage rooms and bike rooms can use a coating or polished concrete. Epoxy and coatings
  • Moisture and salt near the bay and Intracoastal mean moisture testing before any coating.

Balconies and terraces

  • Association-controlled. Balconies are usually common or limited common elements; changes need approval.
  • Often part of repair programs. Balcony slabs are a common focus of milestone repairs; wait until repairs are complete.
  • Waterproofing must be protected; grinding or acid staining can damage it.
  • Slip and heat: use a textured, anti-slip finish, never a high polish.

Aventura's single-family homes and townhomes, including those around Country Club Drive, are on their own slabs and can be polished like any house. In older homes, check under old flooring for terrazzo and test any black adhesive before grinding. Residential polished concrete · Garage floors

Biscayne Boulevard businesses

The Biscayne Boulevard corridor, from the offices and medical plazas to the shops around Aventura Mall and the car dealerships, suits polished concrete well.

  • Medical and dental offices: satin, easy to clean; ask about sealing joints.
  • Retail and showrooms: semi-gloss to high gloss.
  • Car dealerships: polished showroom floors handle tire traffic without peeling.
  • Restaurants: satin with anti-slip; urethane cement in kitchens.
  • Overnight or weekend work so you open as normal.

Common questions

Can I polish the floor in my Aventura condo?

In a unit with another unit below, usually not as a bare polished slab. Florida's building code requires floors between units to reach IIC 50 and STC 50, and many Aventura associations require more. Ground-floor units, lobbies and commercial spaces are different.

There's terrazzo under my carpet. Can I restore it?

Check your association's flooring rules first. Many buildings require carpet or a sound-rated floor in units above others, so exposing terrazzo may not be allowed even though it's original. If it is allowed, restoration is usually worth it.

Our building is going through milestone inspection repairs. Should we redo the floors now?

Structural repairs come first, directed by the building's engineer. Once repairs to corridors, lobbies or garages are finished, that's usually the most sensible time to choose and budget new floor finishes, since those areas are already disrupted.

Can you coat our condo parking garage?

Garage decks in towers often need waterproofing traffic-membrane systems specified by the engineer, which is specialist work. Ground-level garage slabs and storage areas can use epoxy or polished concrete.

What does the association need from a flooring contractor?

Typically a certificate of insurance naming the association, the contractor's licence, a method statement for dust and noise, product data sheets, an elevator booking and work within set hours.

Do you work on houses and townhomes in Aventura?

Yes. Homes and townhomes on their own slab, such as those around Country Club Drive, can be polished like any house.

Which areas do you cover?

All of Aventura (ZIP 33180 and nearby parts of 33160), including Williams Island, Turnberry, Porto Vita and the Biscayne Boulevard corridor, plus Sunny Isles Beach, Golden Beach and North Miami Beach.
