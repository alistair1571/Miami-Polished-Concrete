Older Pinecrest homes

Much of Pinecrest was built out as single-family homes on large lots in the mid-20th century, and many of those homes are now being renovated room by room or opened up into open-plan layouts. Most sit on slabs that polish well, with a few checks first:

  • Asbestos in old adhesive. Black mastic under vinyl tile from before the mid-1980s can contain asbestos. Test before grinding. Asbestos guide
  • Terrazzo under carpet in mid-century homes is often worth restoring. Terrazzo
  • Additions and patches. Decades of extensions and re-plumbing leave slab joins and patches that show once polished. A test patch shows how your slab will look.
  • Opening up walls leaves strips where wall bases sat; they can be patched but may show.
Photo: Open-plan polished floor in a renovated Pinecrest ranch house

New builds and major renovations

Many Pinecrest lots are being rebuilt with large new homes, and polished concrete is often specified from the start. It's far better planned than retrofitted:

  • Before the pour: agree the mix, finishing method and curing with the polisher and builder; these decide how the slab looks polished.
  • Protect the slab during construction from plastic sheeting marks, hydraulic leaks, paint and stored materials.
  • Polish before cabinets and millwork to avoid edge work and give a continuous floor.
  • Exposed aggregate and higher gloss suit large open plans; satin is kinder in kitchens and near pool doors.
  • Tree protection can affect site work and access on some lots; your builder will have to plan around it.

Specifying polished concrete

Indoor-outdoor living

Pinecrest homes often open straight onto patios, loggias and pool areas. Plan the transition:

  • Inside: polished concrete, satin or semi-gloss.
  • Covered loggias: a lower sheen or honed finish, with anti-slip if rain blows in.
  • Open patios and pool decks: textured and slip-resistant, never polished. Pool deck options
  • Matching color: stain or choose an outdoor finish that tones with the interior. Staining

Pools, pool houses and garages

  • Pool houses and cabanas: satin or matte with anti-slip at the doors, because wet feet come straight in.
  • Pool decks: textured, cool and slip-resistant; resurfacing needs a sound slab and a proper coping joint. Pool deck resurfacing
  • Garages: polished concrete or a flake coating, depending on the look and use. Garage floors
  • Separate permits: Pinecrest requires a separate pool permit for pool work.

Tree roots, decks and patios

Pinecrest's mature tree canopy is part of its character, and the Village protects it. Roots are also one of the main reasons pool decks, patios and walkways crack and lift.

  • You need a permit to remove or relocate a tree in Pinecrest, even a dead or diseased one.
  • Cutting major roots can damage or destabilize a tree; get an arborist's advice and check with the Village first.
  • Resurfacing won't fix lifting. Slabs pushed up by roots need repair or replacement before any coating or overlay.
  • Plan construction access around protected trees on new-build and renovation sites.
Photo: Pool deck cracked by tree roots in a Pinecrest garden

Permits in Pinecrest

Pinecrest is a village with its own Building & Planning Department at 12645 Pinecrest Parkway.

  • Separate permits are required for pool, driveway, electrical, plumbing and several other types of work.
  • Interior work: the Village has a minor interior remodeling permit; your contractor should confirm whether your floor project needs one.
  • Trees: removal or relocation needs a permit, which must be issued before any building permit that involves tree work.
  • New construction: signed and sealed plans, tree surveys and landscape compliance documents are part of the process.

US-1 shops, restaurants and offices

Businesses along US-1 (Pinecrest Parkway) and the surrounding commercial strips, including shops, restaurants, salons and medical and professional offices, suit polished concrete well.

  • Offices and clinics: satin salt-and-pepper.
  • Restaurants and cafés: satin with anti-slip; urethane cement in kitchens.
  • Retail and salons: semi-gloss.
  • Weekend or overnight work so you open as normal.

Common questions

How much does polished concrete cost in Pinecrest?

For an existing home after tile removal, a satin finish commonly runs $6 to $9 per sq ft plus $2 to $7 for removal. New builds with exposed aggregate or high gloss typically run $8 to $15 per sq ft, planned before the pour.

Do I need a permit in Pinecrest?

Pinecrest has its own Building & Planning Department. Separate permits are required for pools, driveways and several other types of work, and the Village has a minor interior remodeling permit. Your contractor should confirm what your project needs.

Tree roots are cracking my pool deck. Can I just remove the roots?

Not without care. Pinecrest requires a permit to remove or relocate trees, even dead or diseased ones, and cutting major roots can harm a protected tree. Talk to the Village and an arborist before root work, then repair the deck.

Is polished concrete a good choice for a pool house?

Yes, with a satin or matte finish and anti-slip treatment, because wet feet come straight in from the pool. Keep the pool deck itself textured, not polished.

We're building a new home. When should we plan the polished floors?

Before the slab is poured. The mix, finishing and curing affect the polished result, and the slab should be protected during construction. Polish before cabinets and millwork go in.

My older Pinecrest home has vinyl tile. Anything to check?

Yes. Test any black adhesive under old vinyl or tile for asbestos before grinding, and check for terrazzo under carpet.
