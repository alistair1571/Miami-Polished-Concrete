Hialeah homes

Hialeah was incorporated in 1925, and much of its housing is mid-century concrete block and stucco, built on solid slabs that were later covered with terrazzo, tile, vinyl or carpet. Those slabs are often good candidates for polishing.

  • Asbestos check. Homes built or renovated before the mid-1980s may have black adhesive under old vinyl or tile. Have it lab-tested before anyone grinds. Why
  • Terrazzo check. Some older homes have terrazzo under the carpet. It's usually worth restoring. Terrazzo
  • Patches show. Plumbing trenches and past repairs show once polished. A test patch shows you how your slab will look.
  • Garage conversions and efficiencies are common. Converted garages often sit lower and slope toward the old door. Conversions
Photo: Polished floor in a Hialeah CBS house after tile removal

Tiles popping up? Here's what it usually means

Tiles that crack, sound hollow or lift off the floor are common in Hialeah. Usually the slab isn't the problem: years of humidity and temperature cycling break down the bond between tile, thinset and concrete.

  • Laying new tile over the same slab can lead to the same problem again.
  • Polishing removes the adhesive layer altogether, so there's nothing left to come loose.
  • If the slab has cracks or movement, those are assessed first. Most hairline cracks are cosmetic and can be filled.

Rental properties and landlords

A large share of Hialeah homes are rented, including duplexes, efficiencies and single-family houses. For landlords, polished concrete has real advantages:

  • Nothing to replace between tenants. No carpet to rip out, no vinyl seams to fail.
  • Quick refresh. A deep clean and burnish restores most floors between leases.
  • Pets and wear. Densified concrete handles claws and heavy use better than wood or vinyl plank.
  • Timing. Doing the work between tenants keeps it quick and avoids working around furniture.
  • Several units at once spreads setup costs and lowers the price per foot.

Hialeah warehouses and flex space

Hialeah is one of Miami-Dade's most established industrial areas, with low vacancy and quick access to I-75, the Palmetto Expressway, Okeechobee Road, the airport and PortMiami. Much of the space is flex: office at the front, warehouse behind.

  • Warehouse areas: matte or satin, cream finish, joints filled with polyurea, old striping removed.
  • Office and showroom fronts: satin salt-and-pepper for a finished look.
  • Phased work, nights or weekends, so operations continue.
  • Between tenants is the cheapest time for landlords to upgrade a unit.
  • Slabs that move under trucks need stabilizing before polishing; wet dock doors should stay matte with matting.

Food processing, pharmaceutical and manufacturing floors

Hialeah's industrial tenants include food processors, pharmaceutical companies and light manufacturers. Their floors usually need a split specification:

  • Dry storage, packing, shipping: Usual choice: Polished concrete; Why: Dust-free, durable, easy to clean
  • Offices, labs with light use: Usual choice: Polished concrete, satin; Why: Clean, professional look
  • Wet processing and wash-down: Usual choice: Urethane cement; Why: Hot water, grease and thermal shock
  • Chemical handling, battery charging: Usual choice: Chemical-resistant epoxy; Why: Spill and acid resistance
  • Production areas with oils: Usual choice: Coating or polished with a strong guard; Why: Depends on the fluids used

Regulated facilities should check their own food safety or GMP requirements and ask for safety data sheets for every product. Coatings for industrial areas

Shops, restaurants, clinics and offices

Health care is one of Hialeah's biggest employers, and clinics, dental offices, shops and restaurants all suit polished concrete: it's hard-wearing and easy to keep clean.

  • Clinics and dental offices: satin, easy to clean; ask about sealing joints and cracks for a more cleanable surface.
  • Restaurants and cafeterías: satin with anti-slip near entrances and service counters.
  • Retail: semi-gloss to reflect the lighting.
  • Overnight work so you open as normal.

Concreto pulido en Hialeah: información en español

El concreto pulido convierte la losa de su casa o negocio en el piso terminado: se lija con diamantes, se endurece y se pule hasta el brillo que usted elija. En el sur de la Florida suele costar entre $3 y $12 por pie cuadrado. En casas, un acabado satinado cuesta normalmente entre $6 y $9 por pie cuadrado, más $2 a $7 si hay que quitar losetas.

Antes de empezar

  • ¿Se le están levantando las losetas? Normalmente falla el pegamento, no la losa. Al pulir el concreto, ya no queda pegamento que se despegue.
  • Casas antiguas: si la casa se construyó o se remodeló antes de mediados de los años 80, el pegamento negro debajo del vinil o las losetas puede contener asbesto. Hay que analizarlo en un laboratorio antes de lijar.
  • Terrazo: si encuentra terrazo debajo de la alfombra, casi siempre vale la pena restaurarlo.
  • Condominios: en un apartamento con otro debajo, las normas de ruido de la Florida normalmente no permiten dejar la losa pulida sin aislamiento acústico.
  • Muestra: pida siempre una prueba en su propia losa antes de decidir.

Common questions

How much does polished concrete cost in Hialeah?

Homes with a satin finish after tile removal commonly run $6 to $9 per sq ft, plus $2 to $7 for tile removal. Large warehouse floors price at the low end of Miami's $3 to $8 range.

My tiles are popping up. Can I polish the slab underneath?

Often, yes. Loose or hollow tiles usually mean the adhesive has failed, not the slab. Once the tile and thinset are off, a sound slab can be polished, and there's no adhesive left to fail again. In older homes, test any black adhesive for asbestos first.

Is polished concrete a good choice for a rental property?

It's one of the most durable, low-maintenance floors for rentals: nothing to re-carpet, no grout to clean, and it's quick to refresh between tenants with a clean and burnish.

Can food or pharmaceutical facilities use polished concrete?

In dry storage, packing and office areas, yes. Wet processing, wash-down and sanitary areas usually need a sealed coating such as urethane cement or epoxy.

Can you polish our warehouse while we keep working?

Yes. Work is phased by bay or aisle, or done at nights and weekends.
