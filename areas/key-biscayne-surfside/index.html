Polished Concrete, Microcement and Pool Decks in Key Biscayne and Surfside

Living steps from the ocean is hard on floors: salt air, sand tracked in every day, strong sun, flood-zone rules and condo buildings with strict renovation rules. The right finish here isn't always the one that looks best in a showroom.

What the coast does to floors

  • Sand. Fine beach sand dulls high-gloss floors and scratches coatings. Satin and semi-gloss polished concrete, honed finishes and matte microcement look better for longer. Walk-off mats at every door help more than anything else.
  • Salt air. Salt settles on outdoor concrete, leaves white residue and speeds up corrosion of steel inside concrete. Outdoor sealers need to handle salt and UV.
  • Sun and heat. Ocean-facing pool decks and terraces take intense UV. Use light colors, textured cool finishes and UV-stable sealers.
  • Water from above and below. Both communities are low-lying and largely in coastal flood zones. Floors that can be cleaned and dried recover far better than wood or laminate.

Elevated homes: why polished concrete fits the flood rules

Many new homes on Key Biscayne and in Surfside are built raised above the required flood elevation, with garages, entries and storage at ground level. Under flood-zone rules, spaces below that elevation are generally limited to uses like parking, access and storage, and must be finished with flood-damage-resistant materials. Concrete is one of them, which makes polished or sealed concrete a natural finish for those ground-level spaces.

Renovating an older, lower home? In flood zones, large renovations measured against the home's value can trigger rules requiring the whole home to meet current flood standards (often called the 50% or substantial improvement rule). New floors alone rarely trigger this, but they can be part of a bigger project that does. Your architect, contractor or building department can advise.

Floors on Key Biscayne

  • Older homes: may have terrazzo or old tile over slab. Check before demolition, and test old adhesives for asbestos.
  • New builds: plan polished concrete before the pour, indoors and in ground-level garages and entries. Planning a new slab.
  • Pool decks and terraces: light, textured, UV-stable finishes, rinsed of salt and sand regularly.
  • Condos: association approval, sound-rated underlayment, post-tensioned slabs and limited working hours. Microcement over a sound-rated build-up is usually the route to a seamless look.
  • Getting to the island: every crew, delivery and dumpster comes across the Rickenbacker Causeway. Good quotes allow for causeway traffic and loading-dock times.
  • Approvals: the Village of Key Biscayne has its own building department, and condo associations have their own rules on top.

Floors in Surfside

  • Older homes west of Collins and Harding include many from the 1930s to 1950s, which may hide original terrazzo or period tile. Terrazzo restoration.
  • Rebuilds and elevated homes: polished concrete for ground-level garages and entries, and polished concrete or microcement inside.
  • Oceanfront condos: the same condo rules apply. Microcement is the common route.
  • Harding Avenue shops and restaurants: polished concrete or microcement for retail and dining, urethane cement in kitchens, overnight work.
  • Approvals: the Town of Surfside has its own building department.

Condo balconies, pool decks and garages: when it's not a cosmetic job

After the 2021 Champlain Towers South collapse in Surfside, Florida introduced milestone structural inspections and reserve requirements for condominium buildings. Many coastal associations have since carried out concrete repairs to balconies, pool decks and parking garages.

  • Rust stains, cracks, bulges or spalling on balconies, decks or garage slabs can mean corroding reinforcing steel. That's a job for the association's engineer, not a resurfacing contractor.
  • Cosmetic finishes like microcement, exterior microtoppings or coatings go on only after any structural repairs are done and approved.
  • Traffic and waterproofing coatings on garage and pool decks are engineer-specified systems. See epoxy and traffic coatings.
  • Unit owners usually need association approval before changing balcony floors.

Common questions

What floor finish lasts best near the beach?

Satin polished concrete, restored terrazzo and matte microcement hide sand scratches better than high gloss. Walk-off mats make the biggest difference.

Can I have polished concrete in my Key Biscayne or Surfside condo?

Usually not by grinding the structural slab. Associations require sound-rated underlayment and slabs are often post-tensioned. Microcement over a sound-rated build-up gives the look.

What floors are allowed in the ground level of an elevated home?

Areas below the required flood elevation must use flood-damage-resistant materials. Concrete qualifies, which makes polished or sealed concrete a natural choice. Confirm with your architect and building department.

Will new floors trigger flood elevation requirements?

Flooring alone rarely does, but it can be part of a larger renovation that does. Ask your contractor or building department early.

There's rust coming through my balcony. Can you resurface it?

Not yet. Rust stains and spalling can mean corroding steel inside the concrete. The association's engineer should assess it first; resurfacing comes after repairs.

Does getting to Key Biscayne affect the price?

It can add a little for causeway access, parking and building logistics. A good quote lists it.

Do I need a permit?

Interior floor finishes usually don't need a building permit, but condos need association approval, and exterior or structural work may need permits from the Village of Key Biscayne or the Town of Surfside.
