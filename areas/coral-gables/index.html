Permits and the Board of Architects

Coral Gables controls its look through the Board of Architects, a panel of local architects that meets every week and reviews new homes, pools, pavement, signs and most other exterior changes. For flooring, the key question is whether the work is inside or outside.

  • Interior floors (polishing, staining, microcement): Building department; no Board of Architects review, since the exterior doesn't change
  • Patios, pool decks and walkways: Building permit for resurfacing or changing material; often Board of Architects review
  • Driveways: Permit and Board of Architects review of width, curb cuts and materials
  • Designated historic properties (exterior): Certificate of Appropriateness from historic preservation, regardless of cost
  • Private communities (e.g. Gables Estates): The community's own architectural review may apply as well

Plan the time. Board of Architects approval for exterior work usually takes several weeks. If you're doing inside and outside at once, start the exterior application first.

Sources: City of Coral Gables Board of Architects; Coral Gables permitting guides from Debowsky Design Group, Composite Construction and paversandtiles.com; United Architects on historic review. General guidance only; confirm with Coral Gables Development Services.

Historic homes

Coral Gables has large historic districts and many designated homes, including George Merrick's original 1920s plan and its themed villages. If your home is designated or contributing:

  • Exterior work needs a Certificate of Appropriateness, even for small jobs.
  • Interior flooring is generally handled through the building department, but confirm your property's status before you plan.
  • Original floors such as terrazzo, tile or wood may be worth restoring rather than covering or grinding. Terrazzo restoration
  • Tax exemption: owners who restore a designated property may be eligible for the Historic Preservation Ad Valorem Tax Exemption. Ask the city.

Older Coral Gables homes: check before grinding

  • What's the floor made of? Some older homes have wood floors on a raised structure rather than a slab. Polishing needs a concrete slab.
  • Terrazzo is common in mid-century homes, often hidden under carpet or tile. Restore it rather than grinding it like concrete.
  • Black adhesive under old vinyl or tile in homes renovated before the mid-1980s can contain asbestos. Lab-test it first. Why
  • Patched slabs. Decades of plumbing and remodeling leave patches that show once polished. A test patch shows you how your slab will look.

New builds and luxury homes

In Cocoplum, Gables Estates, Old Cutler and other neighborhoods with large new homes, polished concrete is often specified from the start.

  • Plan it before the pour. Mix, finishing and curing affect how the slab polishes. Bring the polisher in early.
  • Protect the slab during construction. Plastic sheeting, spills and stored materials all show up after polishing.
  • Polish before cabinets and millwork go in, to avoid edge work.
  • Exposed aggregate and higher gloss suit large open plans, but test first and consider satin where floors get wet.

Patios, pool decks and driveways

Pool deck resurfacing options. Outdoor concrete is where Coral Gables rules bite. Resurfacing, staining or changing the material of a pool deck, patio, walkway or driveway usually needs a permit, and the Board of Architects often reviews color and material.

  • Don't polish outdoor concrete to a sheen. It's slippery when wet. Use a textured, sealed or stained finish with anti-slip.
  • Stain and sealer choices for pool decks need to handle sun, heat and pool water. Outdoor staining
  • Get approval before ordering materials, since colors may need to match approved palettes or the home's style.

Condos in Coral Gables

Condo buildings in Downtown Coral Gables and along Ponce de Leon Boulevard follow the same state rules as anywhere else: floors between units must reach IIC 50 and STC 50, so a bare polished slab usually isn't possible in an upper-floor unit. Ground-floor units, lobbies and commercial spaces are different. Condo sound rules · Microcement in condos

Miracle Mile, Downtown Gables and offices

Shops, restaurants and offices along Miracle Mile, Giralda Plaza and Downtown Coral Gables suit polished concrete: durable, quick to clean, and a good match for both modern and traditional interiors.

  • Restaurants: satin with an anti-slip treatment near entrances and bars.
  • Retail and showrooms: semi-gloss to high gloss.
  • Offices: satin salt-and-pepper, which hides scuffs.
  • Overnight or phased work so you open as normal. Interior commercial work goes through the building department; storefront changes involve the Board of Architects.

Common questions

Do I need Board of Architects approval to polish my floors in Coral Gables?

Not for interior floors that don't change the exterior. Interior work goes to the city's building department. Exterior work such as patios, pool decks, walkways and driveways usually needs a permit and often Board of Architects review.

My house is historically designated. Does that affect interior polishing?

Exterior work on a designated property needs a Certificate of Appropriateness regardless of cost. Interior floor work is generally handled through the building department, but check your property's status with the city before you start.

Can I resurface or stain my pool deck in Coral Gables?

Usually, but resurfacing or changing the material of a pool deck, driveway or walkway needs a building permit, and the Board of Architects often reviews color and material. Budget several weeks for approval.

What will I find under the floors in an older Coral Gables house?

It varies by era: original tile, wood, terrazzo in mid-century homes, or a plain slab under later tile or carpet. In homes renovated before the mid-1980s, test any black adhesive for asbestos before grinding.

Can you polish floors in a Coral Gables condo?

In a unit with another unit below, usually not as a bare polished slab, because of Florida's sound rules for floors between units. Ground-floor units, lobbies and commercial spaces are different.

Do you work on Miracle Mile shops and restaurants?

Yes. Retail, restaurant and office floors can be polished overnight or in phases so you open as normal.

Which parts of Coral Gables do you cover?

All of Coral Gables, including the Country Club Section, Granada, Riviera, Old Cutler, Cocoplum, Gables Estates, Gables by the Sea, Snapper Creek, Downtown and Miracle Mile.
