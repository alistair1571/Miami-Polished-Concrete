What's different about floors in Miami Lakes

  • A planned town, built in phases. Homes were built from the 1960s onward, neighborhood by neighborhood. The oldest date from the period when terrazzo was common, often later covered with tile or carpet. Later neighborhoods mostly have slabs under ceramic or porcelain tile.
  • HOAs almost everywhere. Many neighborhoods have associations that control exterior changes like driveways, walkways and visible pool decks. Interior floors usually don't need HOA approval, but condos and townhomes may have hard-floor and sound rules.
  • Life on the lakes. Lakefront patios and pool decks get more moisture, mildew and algae. Outdoor finishes need breathable, mildew-resistant sealers and slip resistance.
  • A business town too. Office parks, medical offices, Main Street retail and restaurants, and flex and light industrial space near the Palmetto Expressway and I-75.
  • The same Miami ground. Porous limestone and a high water table mean moisture moves up through slabs, so coatings need a moisture test first.

Floors for Miami Lakes homes and businesses

1960s and 70s homes

The earliest homes may have terrazzo under later tile or carpet. Check before you demolish, because restoring it can cost less than new flooring. Test old tiles and black adhesives for asbestos before removal.

1980s to 2000s homes and townhomes

Mostly tile over slab. A tear-out and salt-and-pepper polish gives a clean, modern floor through the house. Faint marks from the old tile layout may show, so ask for a test patch. Residential polished concrete.

Condos and townhomes with associations

Check the association's rules for hard floors, sound-rated underlayment and working hours. Upper-floor units often suit microcement better than polishing.

Garages

A flake system with a polyaspartic topcoat is the most popular choice: it resists hot tires and UV, and the flakes hide dust. On damp slabs, a moisture-blocking primer goes down first. Garage floors.

Lakefront pool decks, patios and driveways

Light cool deck or spray knockdown stays comfortable and slip resistant. Use breathable, mildew-resistant sealers, check lifted sections near the lake edge before resurfacing, and get HOA approval for visible color changes. Treat rust from irrigation water at the source. Pool decks · Resurfacing.

Offices, Main Street and flex warehouses

Polished concrete or microcement for offices and clinics, slip-resistant polish for dining rooms, urethane cement in kitchens, and polished concrete with joint filling for flex and warehouse space near the Palmetto and I-75. Commercial polished concrete.

Approvals in Miami Lakes

  • The Town of Miami Lakes has its own building department. Nearby unincorporated areas like Palm Springs North and Country Club fall under Miami-Dade County.
  • HOAs and condo associations: expect approvals for exterior changes and rules for hard floors in condos and townhomes.
  • Interior polishing or coating of an existing slab generally doesn't need a building permit.
  • Commercial tenant spaces: landlord approval and working-hour rules usually apply.

Common questions

Do you cover all of Miami Lakes?

Yes, plus Palm Springs North, Country Club, Hialeah Gardens and surrounding areas.

Do I need HOA approval for new floors?

Usually not for interior floors in a single-family home. Condos and townhomes may have hard-floor and sound rules. Exterior changes like driveways and visible pool decks often need HOA approval.

Could my Miami Lakes home have terrazzo?

Homes from the town's early years may. Lift a carpet corner or a tile in a closet to check, or ask the contractor to look.

What's the best garage floor for Miami Lakes?

A full-flake system with a polyaspartic topcoat. It resists hot tires and UV and hides dust. A moisture test first prevents peeling.

How do I stop mildew on my lakefront pool deck?

Clean and treat it, then resurface with a light, textured finish and a breathable, mildew-resistant sealer.

Do you do offices and warehouses in Miami Lakes?

Yes. Polished concrete and epoxy for offices, clinics, retail, restaurants and flex or warehouse space, usually after hours.

Do I need a permit?

Interior polishing or coating usually doesn't. Where permits are needed, they go through the Town of Miami Lakes, or Miami-Dade County for nearby unincorporated areas.
