What's under the floors in Miami Shores

Miami Shores was developed from the 1920s, with Mediterranean Revival homes from the early boom years and then waves of 1930s to 60s ranch and mid-century homes. That era put a lot of terrazzo into Miami homes: durable, cool and made locally, with marble chips set in cement and divided by metal strips. Later owners covered much of it with carpet, tile or laminate.

  • A carpet or tile tear-out often uncovers terrazzo, sometimes in excellent condition, sometimes damaged by tack strips, glue and thinset.
  • Restoring original terrazzo can cost less than new flooring and keeps the home's period character.
  • Some older homes have slab floors that can be polished, and some earlier homes have raised wood floors with no slab at all.
  • Old floor tiles and black adhesives from this period can contain asbestos. Test before anyone grinds or scrapes. See the asbestos guide.

How to check for terrazzo yourself

  1. Lift a carpet corner inside a closet or behind a door and look at the floor underneath.
  2. Look at doorways and thresholds. Terrazzo sometimes shows where two floors meet.
  3. Check under the AC closet or water heater if it sits on the original floor.
  4. Look for divider strips: thin brass or zinc lines in a grid or pattern.
  5. Under tile it's harder to tell. A contractor can lift one tile in an out-of-sight spot.

Found speckled stone chips set in a smooth base? That's likely terrazzo. Send us a photo with your details.

What terrazzo restoration involves

  1. Test adhesives and old tiles for asbestos where needed
  2. Remove carpet, tack strips, tile, thinset and glue
  3. Repair tack-strip holes and cracks, and patch missing areas with matched chips
  4. Grind to flatten lippage and remove deep scratches and residue
  5. Hone and polish through finer diamond grits
  6. Seal with a penetrating sealer, with no wax build-up

Honest limits: patches never match perfectly, deep cracks can show after restoration, and terrazzo removed for past wall changes leaves gaps to fill. Ask to see samples before patching large areas. Avoid anyone who quotes to "restore" terrazzo by just waxing it: wax yellows and traps dirt, where mechanical polishing refines the surface itself.

Miami Shores rules to know

  • Miami Shores is its own village, with its own building and zoning department, separate from the City of Miami.
  • Exterior appearance: the Village is known for design standards on exterior changes. Check before changing driveways, walkways or other visible surfaces.
  • Interior restoration or polishing generally doesn't need a building permit, but your contractor should confirm.

Common questions

Is there terrazzo under my carpet in Miami Shores?

There's a good chance in homes built from the 1940s to the 60s. Lift a carpet corner in a closet to check, or send us a photo.

Is it worth restoring old terrazzo?

Usually. It often costs less than new flooring, lasts for decades more, and keeps the home's original character.

Can damaged terrazzo be repaired?

Cracks, tack-strip holes and missing sections can be repaired with matched chips, but patches won't be a perfect match. Samples help you decide.

Does old terrazzo contain asbestos?

Terrazzo itself typically doesn't, but old floor tiles and black adhesives laid over it can. Test before removal.

Can I polish the concrete if there's no terrazzo?

Yes, if there's a sound slab. Some early homes have raised wood floors with no slab.

Do I need a permit in Miami Shores?

Interior restoration or polishing usually doesn't. Exterior changes like driveways and walkways may need Village review.

How long does terrazzo restoration take?

Typically three to five days for an average home, longer with heavy glue removal or large repairs.

How do I maintain restored terrazzo?

Dust mop, clean with a pH-neutral cleaner, avoid vinegar and bleach, and skip wax. Burnish or re-seal every few years.
