What's different about floors in Homestead

A newer housing stock than most of Miami-Dade. Hurricane Andrew devastated Homestead in 1992, and much of the city was rebuilt or built new afterwards, followed by rapid subdivision growth from the 2000s onward. Most Homestead homes sit on relatively modern slabs under builder-grade tile. That usually means a more even slab after tear-out than older homes further north, and good odds for a clean salt-and-pepper polish.

A shallow water table over limestone. South Dade sits on porous limestone with groundwater close to the surface. Moisture moves up through slabs even when they look dry. That's the main reason garage and patio coatings bubble and peel here, and why every coating quote should start with a moisture test.

Well water and rust stains. Many Redland and rural properties, and some irrigation systems in town, run on well water high in iron. The result is orange rust stains on driveways, walkways, pool decks and patios. They can be removed and resurfaced over, but they'll come back unless the source is fixed.

Working buildings. Packing houses, nurseries, cold storage, equipment barns, auto shops and warehouses along the US-1 and Turnpike corridors need floors that handle produce, water, chemicals and machinery, often with the building still running.

Floors for Homestead homes and businesses

New-build and subdivision homes

The most common Homestead project: pulling up builder tile and polishing the slab underneath, or coating the garage. Newer slabs often polish evenly, but tile layouts can leave faint ghost marks, so ask for a test patch. Many HOA communities control exterior work like driveways and walkways. Garages suit a full-flake system with a polyaspartic topcoat, with a moisture-blocking primer on damp slabs.

Older Homestead homes and downtown buildings

Homes and commercial buildings around historic downtown Homestead may predate Andrew. Expect more patches, cracks and old adhesives, and test pre-mid-1980s adhesives for asbestos before grinding. Some may hide original terrazzo.

Redland and rural properties

Barns and workshops suit polished concrete, or a chemical-resistant epoxy for equipment, fuel and fertilizer areas. Patios, driveways and pool decks suit resurfacing once rust from well water is dealt with.

Agriculture: packing houses, nurseries and cold storage

  • Packing and processing areas: urethane cement or slip-resistant quartz systems that handle water, produce acids and wash-downs, with a cove base where walls meet floors.
  • Cold storage and coolers: systems rated for low temperatures. Standard epoxy becomes brittle.
  • Nursery and greenhouse buildings: densifying, or polished concrete for work areas.
  • Equipment and tractor shops: oil-resistant coatings with slip resistance.
  • Harvest season: plan installs around the growing calendar. Winter is peak season for many South Dade crops.

Warehouses, retail, auto and hospitality

Distribution and storage near the Turnpike and US-1, retail along Campbell Drive, dealerships and service bays, and Florida City hotels and restaurants serving Everglades and Keys traffic. Polished concrete for warehouses and showrooms, oil-resistant coatings for service bays, urethane cement for kitchens, and overnight phasing around guests and trading hours.

Permits, HOAs and who to ask

  • City of Homestead and Florida City are separate municipalities, each with its own building department.
  • Unincorporated areas like the Redland, Leisure City, Naranja, Princeton and Goulds fall under Miami-Dade County.
  • Interior polishing or coating of an existing slab generally doesn't need a building permit, but structural repairs, new pours or right-of-way driveway work may.
  • Many Homestead subdivisions require HOA approval for driveway, walkway and other exterior changes.

Common questions

Do you cover Homestead, Florida City and the Redland?

Yes, along with Leisure City, Naranja, Princeton, Goulds and the rest of South Dade.

Is there a travel charge for Homestead?

Any travel or minimum charge is stated in your written quote before you agree to anything.

Can I polish the slab under my builder tile?

Usually, yes. Most Homestead homes have relatively modern slabs that polish well. Faint marks from the tile layout can show, and a test patch shows you what to expect.

Why do garage coatings peel in Homestead?

Mostly moisture pushing up from the shallow water table, plus poor prep and hot tires. A moisture test, diamond grinding and a moisture-blocking primer prevent most failures.

How do I get rid of orange rust stains on my driveway?

They usually come from iron in well or irrigation water. Rust removers clean them and resurfacing covers them, but fix the water source or redirect sprinklers, or they'll return.

What's the best floor for a packing house or cold room?

Usually urethane cement, which handles wash-downs, produce acids, hot water and cold temperatures. Quartz broadcast systems suit drier food-handling areas.

Do I need a permit?

Interior polishing or coating usually doesn't. Permits are handled by the City of Homestead, Florida City or Miami-Dade County depending on where you are. HOAs may need to approve exterior work.

When's the best time for outdoor work?

The dry season, roughly November to April. Farms may prefer the summer off-season for work buildings.
