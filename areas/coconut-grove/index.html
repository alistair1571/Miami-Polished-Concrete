What's different about floors in Coconut Grove

Low ground by the bay. Much of the Grove sits only a few feet above sea level, and bayside streets have flooded in storm surge and during seasonal king tides. On ground floors near the water, polished concrete, microcement over concrete and epoxy can usually be cleaned and dried, where wood, laminate and carpet are torn out. FEMA guidance lists concrete among flood-damage-resistant materials.

The canopy. The Grove's big oaks, banyans and ficus lift driveways and walkways with their roots, drop leaves that leave brown tannin stains on pool decks and patios, and keep outdoor slabs shaded and damp, which means mildew.

Not every Grove home has a slab. Many older homes, especially historic cottages and some bungalows, are built on raised wood floors over a crawl space. There's no slab to polish. It's worth checking before you plan around polished concrete.

Floors for Grove homes, condos and businesses

Modern new builds and major remodels

Many new Grove homes are designed with polished concrete floors from the start, often high-gloss or salt-and-pepper, running indoors to covered terraces. The best results come from planning the polish before the slab is poured. On flood-prone lots, polished concrete on a ground floor is one of the most resilient finishes you can choose.

Mid-century ranch homes

Tile or carpet over the original slab, and sometimes original terrazzo underneath. Restoring terrazzo can cost less and add character. Test pre-mid-1980s adhesives for asbestos before grinding.

Historic homes and coral rock houses

Raised wood floors, historic materials and sometimes historic designation. Polishing isn't usually an option on wood-framed floors. For designated historic properties, check with the City of Miami's historic preservation office before changing visible exterior surfaces.

Waterfront condos and towers

Expect written association approval, sound-rated underlayment, post-tensioned slabs and working-hour limits. Microcement over a sound-rated build-up is often the practical route to a seamless look.

Restaurants, retail and marinas

Around CocoWalk, Main Highway, Grand Avenue and the Center Grove: polished concrete or microcement in dining rooms and bars, urethane cement in kitchens, and textured, slip-resistant outdoor seating. Marina buildings along the bay need floors that handle saltwater, fuel and wash-downs.

Approvals in Coconut Grove

  • The Grove is part of the City of Miami, so the City of Miami building department handles any permits.
  • Some Grove homes and areas carry historic designation or conservation rules.
  • The City of Miami regulates removal of many trees.
  • Interior polishing or coating of an existing slab generally doesn't need a building permit.

Common questions

Can I polish the floors in my older Coconut Grove home?

Only if there's a concrete slab. Many older Grove homes have raised wood floors over a crawl space, which can't be polished. A contractor can check quickly.

Is polished concrete a good choice if my street floods?

It's one of the most resilient. It can usually be cleaned, dried and re-polished after water intrusion, where wood and laminate are replaced.

How do I stop tree roots cracking my driveway?

Resurfacing won't fix lifted concrete. The damaged sections need replacing and the roots managing. Check whether you need a City of Miami tree permit first.

How do I remove brown leaf stains from my pool deck?

Tannin stains come out with suitable cleaners. After that, a sealed, light-colored textured finish makes them less noticeable.

Can I have polished concrete in my Grove condo?

Ground floors often, yes. Upper floors need association approval and sound-rated underlayment, and slabs may be post-tensioned. Microcement is often the better route.

Do I need a permit?

Interior polishing or coating usually doesn't need a building permit. Historic properties, tree work and structural work may need approvals from the City of Miami.

Do you work with restaurants in the Grove?

Yes. Dining rooms, bars, kitchens and outdoor seating, usually done overnight so you don't close.
