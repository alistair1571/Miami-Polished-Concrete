Garage Floor Epoxy in Miami

A coated garage floor wipes clean of oil, sand and tire marks and makes the whole house feel finished. But Miami is one of the hardest places to keep a garage coating stuck down: heat, humidity, damp slabs, hot tires and UV through an open door. Here's which systems last here, what they cost, and what a proper install involves.

How much does it cost to coat a garage floor in Miami?

A professional flake coating system for a two-car garage typically costs about $2,900 to $4,800 in Florida. Polishing a 400 sq ft garage is about $1,600 to $2,700. Metallic epoxy costs more, and removing an old coating or treating a damp slab adds cost. See pricing.

Why garage coatings fail in Miami, and how to avoid it

  1. Moisture coming up through the slab. Many Miami garage slabs sit close to the water table, and older homes often have no vapor barrier under the garage. Moisture gets trapped under the coating and causes bubbles and peeling. Fix: test before quoting; use a moisture-mitigating primer or choose polished concrete. How moisture testing works.
  2. Poor prep. DIY kits and cheap installers acid etch the floor, which doesn't remove old sealers, oil or weak surface concrete. Fix: diamond grinding.
  3. Hot tire pickup. Hot tires soften weak coatings and pull them up. Fix: a professional system with a hard polyaspartic or polyurethane topcoat.
  4. Humidity during install. High humidity can cause amine blush on epoxy, a waxy film that stops the next coat bonding. Fix: check humidity and dew point, work early, use humidity-tolerant topcoats.
  5. UV through an open door. Many epoxies yellow in sunlight. Fix: a UV-stable topcoat.

Choosing a flake floor

  • Full broadcast: flakes cover the entire floor. The most durable and slip resistant, and the best at hiding dirt. Strongly recommended for Miami garages.
  • Partial broadcast: flakes scattered over a colored base. Cheaper, but shows more dirt and wears faster.
  • Flake size: 1/4" is the standard look; 1/8" gives a finer, granite-like finish.
  • Color: gray, tan and black-white-gray blends hide sand, dust and tire marks best.
  • Texture: anti-slip grit in the topcoat is worth it if the floor gets wet from rain, pool bags or washing the car.

What a professional installation looks like

  1. Clear the garage. Some contractors include moving items; ask.
  2. Moisture test the slab before any coating goes down.
  3. Diamond grind the whole floor with vacuum dust extraction.
  4. Repair cracks, pits and spalls, and honor or fill control joints.
  5. Stem walls: the low curb around the garage walls can be coated for a clean edge.
  6. Base coat, with moisture-mitigating primer if needed.
  7. Flakes or color broadcast into the wet base, then scraped and vacuumed.
  8. Clear topcoat, hot-tire and UV resistant, with anti-slip grit if chosen.

Garage gyms, workshops and conversions

  • Home gym: a flake floor under cardio equipment is fine. Use rubber mats in free-weight areas, as dropped weights can chip any coating.
  • Workshop: a thicker system with high chemical resistance for solvents and brake fluid.
  • Detailing: full flake or solid with anti-slip grit makes washing easier. Metallic shows swirl marks.
  • Converted garages: the floor is usually a step down and sloped toward the door. For living space, polished concrete or microcement looks more like a room than a flake coating, and the conversion itself may need permits from your city.

Hurricanes and flooding

A coated garage floor cleans up far more easily after water gets in: mud, silt and saltwater don't soak into the surface. But a coating doesn't flood-proof a garage. After a flood, clean, dry and ventilate, and have any bubbled or lifted areas checked. Polished concrete has nothing to lift, so it's the lower-risk choice on flood-prone streets.

Care, warranties and questions to ask

  • Sweep regularly; sand scratches over time.
  • Wash with a mild, pH-neutral cleaner. Avoid citrus and harsh degreasers on the topcoat.
  • Wipe up oil, brake fluid and battery acid promptly.
  • Use pads under kickstands and jack stands.

What a warranty should cover

Covered: peeling or delamination from poor bonding, and hot tire pickup. Often excluded: moisture failures without mitigation, impact and chemical damage, slab cracks that move, and UV yellowing on epoxy-only topcoats. Get it in writing, including whether it transfers when you sell.

Questions to ask before hiring

  1. Is this 100% solids epoxy, polyaspartic or water-based, and how thick?
  2. Will you diamond grind, or acid etch?
  3. Will you test the slab for moisture first?
  4. Is the topcoat UV-stable and hot-tire resistant?
  5. Full or partial flake broadcast?
  6. What happens to cracks, joints and stem walls?
  7. When can I park my car?
  8. What does the warranty cover and exclude?
  9. Can I see garages you did two or more years ago?

Common questions

How much does it cost to epoxy a two-car garage in Miami?

A professional flake coating system typically runs about $2,900 to $4,800 in Florida for a two-car garage. Polishing a 400 sq ft garage is about $1,600 to $2,700. Old coating removal and moisture treatment add cost.

Epoxy or polyaspartic for a Miami garage?

Many of the best systems use both: an epoxy base for build and bonding, and a polyaspartic topcoat for UV stability and hot tire resistance. Full polyaspartic systems cure fastest.

Why is my epoxy garage floor peeling?

Usually poor prep (acid etching instead of grinding), moisture coming up through the slab, or hot tire pickup on a soft coating. The failed coating must be removed before recoating.

What is hot tire pickup?

When hot tires soften a weak coating and pull it off the floor when you drive away. Professional systems with hard topcoats resist it.

How long before I can park on it?

About 24 to 48 hours for polyaspartic systems, and about 5 to 7 days for most epoxy systems. Polished garages can be driven on soon after they're finished.

Are DIY epoxy kits worth it in Miami?

They're cheap upfront, but they rely on acid etching and thin coats, and they often fail in Miami's heat, humidity and with hot tires.

Is an epoxy garage floor slippery?

A smooth gloss coating can be slippery when wet. Flakes and anti-slip grit in the topcoat make it much safer.

Will it hide cracks?

Flakes hide stains and minor imperfections well. Cracks are repaired first, but moving cracks can reappear through any coating.

How long does an epoxy garage floor last?

A well-installed professional system typically lasts 10 to 20 years in a residential garage, with a fresh topcoat sometimes needed for appearance. DIY kits often last 1 to 3 years.

Will it turn yellow?

Epoxy-only floors can yellow in sunlight. A UV-stable polyaspartic or polyurethane topcoat prevents it.

Is polished concrete better than epoxy for a garage?

Polished concrete can't peel and suits damp slabs, but it's less resistant to oil stains. Epoxy and flake systems resist oil and chemicals better and hide imperfections.

Can I coat or polish a converted garage?

Yes. Converted garages usually need the step down to the house addressed and may need permits for the conversion itself. Polished concrete or microcement suits living space; flake coatings look more like a garage.
