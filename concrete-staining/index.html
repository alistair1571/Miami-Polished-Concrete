Acid Stained Concrete in Miami

Acid stain turns plain gray concrete into a marbled, translucent floor with the depth of natural stone, and the color is permanent because it becomes part of the concrete. It's also the decorative finish most likely to surprise you. Here's how to know what your slab will look like, which stain and sealer suit Miami, and what it costs.

What is acid stained concrete?

Acid stain isn't a paint or a coating. It's a solution of water, metallic salts and a mild acid, usually hydrochloric. When it's applied to bare concrete, the acid opens up the surface and the metallic salts react with the lime (calcium hydroxide) in the concrete to form new colored compounds inside it.

Because every slab has different amounts of lime in different places, the color develops unevenly, giving the mottled, veined look people love. The color won't chip, peel or flake off, because there is nothing on top to peel. What does wear is the sealer or guard applied over it, which is why sealer choice matters so much in Miami.

Will acid stain look good on my concrete?

This is the most important question, and most sites avoid it. Acid stain doesn't hide anything. It reacts with whatever is in the surface, so it tends to amplify what's already there.

Things that will show through

  • Glue, thinset and tape residue from old flooring
  • The layout of old tile
  • Patches, repairs and filled cracks
  • Trowel marks, burn marks and swirl patterns
  • Drywall mud, paint, oil and rust marks
  • Carpet tack strip holes
  • Curing compounds or old sealers never removed

Things that stop it working

  • Sealers and coatings. Acid stain can't react through them. They must be fully removed.
  • Densified or heavily polished surfaces. Stain before densifying, not after.
  • Some overlays and patching products don't contain enough lime to react.

A simple test you can do today

Sprinkle water on your concrete. If it darkens and soaks in within a minute or so, the surface is open enough to take stain. If it beads up, there's a sealer or residue that has to come off first.

Always ask for a test sample on your own slab, sealed with the real sealer. The color right after staining looks chalky and dull, and it changes dramatically once the residue is washed off and the sealer goes on. Judge the sealed sample, never the wet stain.

Acid stain colors, and popular Miami looks

Acid stains come in a limited, natural palette because they're made from metallic salts: browns and tans, amber and gold, terra cotta and reds, greens and blue-greens, and black. Colors can be layered for depth. For bright colors, whites or exact grays, use a water-based stain or dye.

Where stained concrete works best

  • Interior floors: living areas, kitchens, restaurants, retail and offices. The most durable version is stained polished concrete: the stain goes in during polishing, then it's densified and guarded, with no topical sealer to wear. See polished concrete.
  • Covered patios, lanais and Florida rooms: a great fit, protected from direct rain and much of the UV.
  • Uncovered patios and walkways: works well with a breathable, UV-stable sealer and a slip-resistant additive.
  • Pool decks: possible, but a sealed, stained deck can be slippery when wet and dark colors get very hot. For most Miami decks, a textured cool deck is safer. See pool deck resurfacing.
  • Driveways and carports: the sealer takes a beating from hot tires, oil and rain. Expect more frequent resealing.
  • Concrete countertops: can be acid stained and sealed with a food-safe countertop sealer.

The sealer is what actually matters in Miami

The stain is permanent. The sealer isn't. Most stained concrete complaints in South Florida are really sealer problems:

  • Whitening or cloudiness (blushing). Moisture gets trapped under or inside a sealer and it turns milky white. Very common outdoors in Miami's humidity and afternoon rain.
  • Peeling and flaking. Too much sealer, sealer over a damp slab, or the wrong sealer for outdoors.
  • Slipperiness. Glossy sealers get slick when wet. Outdoor sealers should include an anti-slip additive.
  • Yellowing. Some sealers, especially many epoxies, yellow in sun.
  • Wear in traffic lanes. Interior sealers wear where people walk most.

What works better in Miami

  • Outdoors: breathable, UV-stable sealers applied thin, to fully dry concrete, with anti-slip grit. Plan on resealing every 1 to 3 years.
  • Indoors: stained polished concrete with a penetrating guard, or a durable urethane or polyaspartic topcoat where you want more gloss.
  • Timing: avoid sealing right before or after afternoon storms. The dry season is easiest for outdoor work.

How acid staining is done

  1. Assessment and sample. The contractor checks porosity, residue and condition, and stains and seals a small sample.
  2. Prep. The surface is cleaned, and old sealers, glue and coatings are removed. New concrete should cure for about 28 days first.
  3. Protect. Walls, baseboards, cabinets, metal, stone, plants and pool coping are masked. Acid stain will etch or discolor anything it touches.
  4. Apply. Stain is sprayed, brushed or sponged on, often in layers.
  5. Let it react. Usually several hours, sometimes overnight.
  6. Neutralize and clean. The residue is neutralized, scrubbed and rinsed until the water runs clear. Residue left behind causes sealer failure.
  7. Dry. The slab must dry fully, which can take longer in Miami's humidity.
  8. Seal or polish. A sealer goes on in thin coats, or the floor is densified, polished and guarded.

Most jobs take 2 to 4 days including drying. Foot traffic can usually return 24 to 48 hours after sealing. Rinse water and residue must be neutralized and collected, never washed into storm drains that flow to Biscayne Bay and local canals.

Acid stain pros and cons

Pros

  • Permanent color that won't chip, peel or flake
  • Unique, stone-like depth no coating can match
  • Uses your existing concrete
  • Very durable, especially as stained polished concrete
  • UV-stable color outdoors
  • Easy to clean

Cons

  • Unpredictable. No two floors match the sample exactly.
  • Limited, earth-tone palette
  • Highlights flaws instead of hiding them
  • The sealer needs maintenance, especially outdoors
  • Can be darkened but not lightened
  • Hazardous products that need professional handling

Already have stained concrete that looks bad?

  • White, cloudy patches: trapped moisture in the sealer. It can often be revived with solvent or stripped and redone.
  • Peeling sealer: stripped and resealed with a product suited to the location.
  • Faded traffic lanes: cleaned, re-stained where needed and resealed, or converted to stained polished concrete for good.
  • A color you don't like: can usually be darkened with a dye or second stain. Lightening means grinding it off.
  • Slippery patio: resealed with an anti-slip additive.

Caring for stained concrete

  • Dust mop or sweep regularly. Grit wears the sealer.
  • Damp mop with a pH-neutral cleaner. No vinegar, bleach, ammonia or citrus.
  • Wipe spills quickly. Use felt pads and walk-off mats.
  • Reseal outdoor areas every 1 to 3 years and interior sealed floors as they wear.

Common questions

Is acid stained concrete permanent?

The color is. It reacts with the concrete and becomes part of it. The sealer on top wears and needs renewing, especially outdoors.

Why does my stained floor look different from the photos?

Every slab reacts differently depending on its lime content, age, finish and any residue. That's why a sealed sample on your own concrete is essential.

Can you acid stain old concrete?

Yes, if it's clean and porous. Old sealers, glue and coatings must be removed first, and existing marks will usually show through.

Can you acid stain after removing tile?

You can, but the old tile layout and thinset marks often show clearly. A water-based stain, dye or overlay gives a more even result.

Acid stain or water-based stain?

Acid stain for natural, marbled, stone-like depth in earth tones. Water-based stain for even color, modern grays and a wider palette.

Is stained concrete slippery?

The stain doesn't change slip resistance, but a glossy sealer can make it slippery when wet. Outdoors, use an anti-slip additive.

Why is my stained patio turning white?

Usually moisture trapped in or under the sealer, a common issue in Miami humidity. It can often be revived or stripped and resealed with a breathable sealer.

How long does stained concrete last?

The color lasts as long as the concrete. Sealers last about 1 to 3 years outdoors and longer indoors. Stained polished concrete needs the least upkeep.

Can stained concrete be polished?

Yes. Staining or dyeing during the polishing process gives the most durable interior finish with no topical sealer.

Can I stain my pool deck?

You can, but stained and sealed decks can be slippery and dark colors get hot. Most Miami owners are better off with a cool deck or knockdown finish.

Is acid stain safe?

Once neutralized, rinsed and sealed, yes. During application it gives off fumes and must be handled by professionals, with pets and kids kept away.

How long before I can walk on it?

Usually 24 to 48 hours after sealing for foot traffic, longer for furniture and cars.
