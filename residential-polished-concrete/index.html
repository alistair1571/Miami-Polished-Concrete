Is polished concrete right for your home?

It's usually a great fit if:

  • You're replacing tile, carpet, laminate or vinyl on a ground-floor slab
  • You want one continuous floor through the house with no grout lines
  • You have kids, dogs, a pool or a beach habit, and want a floor you can just mop
  • You want something that handles humidity and water better than wood or laminate
  • You like a modern, minimal, industrial or coastal look

It may need a different approach if:

  • You live in an upper-floor condo. Sound rules and post-tensioned slabs usually make microcement the practical choice.
  • You want a soft, warm floor everywhere. Concrete is hard. Rugs help, but it won't feel like carpet.
  • You want a perfectly uniform look on an old slab. A polished overlay or microcement gives a consistent look.
  • The slab has serious moisture or structural cracking. That gets fixed first.

What to expect in your type of Miami home

1950s to 1960s ranch and mid-century homes

Common in Miami Shores, North Miami, Coral Gables, Westchester and Miami Beach. Many have original terrazzo under the carpet or tile. Check before choosing polished concrete, because restoring terrazzo is often the better move. See terrazzo restoration. These homes may also have old black adhesive, which should be tested for asbestos.

1970s to 1990s block homes with tile

The most common Miami project. Tile and thinset come off, the slab is ground and polished. Expect some ghost marks from the old tile layout. Many owners like the character; if not, a dye can soften them. Pre-mid-1980s adhesives should be tested.

Newer homes and townhomes

Slabs from the 2000s onward are usually in good condition, so cream and salt-and-pepper finishes are more achievable.

New builds, additions and ADUs

The best possible result, because the slab can be poured with polishing in mind. Talk to your builder before the pour. See planning a new slab.

Condos and apartments

Ground-floor units can often be polished. Upper floors usually need association approval, a sound-rated build-up and a thin finish rather than grinding into the structure. See microcement for condos.

What's under your floor matters

  • Tile: tile and thinset are removed, usually with a mechanical scraper and grinder to limit dust. The pattern of the old tile layout may faintly show through.
  • Carpet: carpet comes up easily, but tack strip holes around the room's edge leave a line of small holes. They're filled, but they can still show. Borders, rugs or a darker dye near the walls hide them well.
  • Glue-down wood, vinyl or VCT: old glue can stain the concrete. Pre-mid-1980s black adhesive must be tested for asbestos before grinding. See the asbestos guide.
  • Terrazzo: stop. Don't grind terrazzo into concrete. Restore it. See terrazzo restoration.
  • Paint, sealer or epoxy: removed in the first grinding passes.

Faint marks after a tear-out are the most common surprise. A test patch on your own slab shows you what to expect before you commit.

Polished concrete room by room

Living and dining areas

The most popular rooms. A single continuous floor makes open-plan Miami homes feel bigger and brighter. Satin or semi-gloss is the usual choice. Add large rugs for comfort and acoustics.

Kitchens

Hard-wearing and easy to clean. It's hard on dropped glasses and on your feet if you cook for hours, so an anti-fatigue mat at the sink helps. Wipe acidic spills like lime juice, vinegar and wine quickly to avoid etching. Existing cabinets are polished around, with edges done by hand, which can finish slightly differently from the open floor. If you're remodeling, polish before the new cabinets go in.

Bedrooms

Cool in summer, allergy-friendly, and rugs make them feel softer.

Entryways and hallways

Sand from the beach and yard is the main thing that dulls polished floors. Use a walk-off mat inside the door.

Bathrooms

Polished concrete can work on bathroom floors with a slip-resistant guard. For showers, walls and wet zones, microcement is the better choice because it's designed to be waterproofed.

Garages

Polished concrete makes a durable garage floor that won't peel from hot tires. Compare garage floor options.

Florida rooms, lanais and covered patios

Covered areas can be polished. Uncovered patios and pool decks get too slippery when wet, so resurfacing is the better choice.

Living with polished concrete

  • Dogs and cats. Claws won't scratch a polished floor the way they scratch wood or vinyl, accidents clean up without soaking in, and fur sweeps straight up. A satin finish shows less and gives a bit more grip for older dogs.
  • Kids. It stands up to toys, spills, scooters and crayons. It's hard, so rugs in play areas are a good idea.
  • Allergies. No grout lines or carpet fibers means nowhere for dust mites, pollen or mold to settle.
  • Aging in place. One continuous floor with no grout lines, raised thresholds or trip edges is easier for walkers and wheelchairs. Ask for a slip-resistant guard.
  • Comfort. It's cool underfoot, a win for most of the Miami year. Area rugs, runners and kitchen mats solve the hardness.
  • Noise. Hard floors reflect sound, so large open rooms can feel echoey. Rugs, curtains and soft furniture fix it.

Choosing a look for your home

Three choices shape how your floor looks. Your contractor should show you a test patch on your own slab before you commit.

  • How much stone shows: smooth cream, salt and pepper (the most popular for homes), medium aggregate or full aggregate for a terrazzo-like look.
  • How much it shines: flat, satin, semi-gloss or high gloss. Satin and semi-gloss are most practical for homes; high gloss shows every grain of sand.
  • Color: natural gray, dyed (warm grays, browns, charcoal, black), acid stained for a marbled look, or saw-cut into patterns and borders.

What happens in your home during the job

Before the job

  • Clear the rooms of furniture, rugs and anything on the floor. Some contractors offer furniture moving; ask.
  • Baseboards usually come off so the grinder can reach the edges, then get reinstalled or replaced. Ask whether that's in the quote.
  • Interior doors may need trimming if the new floor sits at a different height.
  • Plan for pets to stay elsewhere during grinding days.

Typical timeline for a 1,500 to 2,000 sq ft home

DayWhat happens
1Tile, carpet or flooring removal and cleanup
2Grinding and repairs to cracks and holes
3Densifier, then polishing passes (plus dye if chosen)
4Final polish, stain guard and burnish
5Baseboards reinstalled, furniture back

Can you stay at home?

Usually yes, in rooms not being worked on. Grinding is loud during working hours. Good contractors use HEPA dust extraction attached to the machines, so dust is minimal, but seal off rooms and cover electronics anyway.

After the job

You can walk on it straight away. Furniture goes back after about 24 hours, with felt pads under every leg. Hold off on area rugs for a few days so the guard can fully cure.

Why it works so well in Miami homes

  • Humidity: doesn't swell, cup or gap like wood and laminate.
  • Heat: stays cool underfoot through the summer.
  • Hurricane season and water: after a leak or flood, polished concrete can be cleaned and dried rather than torn out. It isn't waterproof, so water still needs cleaning up quickly.
  • Sand and salt: easy to sweep, won't hold grit in grout lines.
  • Brighter rooms: a polished floor reflects daylight, so rooms feel noticeably brighter.
  • Mold: no carpet, underlay or grout for mold to grow in.

Does polished concrete add value to a Miami home?

It depends on the buyer and the neighborhood. In modern homes, new builds and design-led areas like Wynwood, Edgewater, Coconut Grove and Miami Beach, polished concrete reads as a premium, low-maintenance feature. In more traditional homes, some buyers prefer tile or wood. If resale is a priority, a neutral color and a satin or semi-gloss finish have the broadest appeal.

Keeping your floor looking new

  • Dust mop or vacuum (hard-floor setting) regularly. Sand is the main enemy.
  • Damp mop weekly with a pH-neutral cleaner made for polished concrete.
  • Never use vinegar, bleach, citrus or ammonia cleaners, or wax.
  • Wipe spills fast, especially lime, wine, coffee, oils and pet accidents.
  • Walk-off mats at every door. Felt pads under furniture.
  • A light re-polish or fresh guard every few years keeps it like new.

Common questions

Can you polish concrete under my tile?

Yes, in most Miami homes. The tile and thinset are removed and the slab is ground and polished. Faint marks from the old tile layout may show. A test patch shows you before you commit.

What about the holes from carpet tack strips?

They're filled, but may still show as a faint line around the edge of the room. Rugs, a border or a darker dye hide them well.

How long does it take to do a whole house?

Most 1,500 to 2,000 sq ft homes take 4 to 6 days including tile removal.

Can we live in the house during the work?

Usually, yes. You'll need to stay out of the rooms being worked on. It's loud during working hours, but HEPA dust extraction keeps dust to a minimum.

Is polished concrete good with dogs?

Very. Claws won't scratch it the way they scratch wood or vinyl, and accidents don't soak in. Satin finishes give a bit more grip.

Is polished concrete cold or uncomfortable?

It's cool, which suits Miami, and hard like tile. Area rugs and kitchen mats make it comfortable.

Is it slippery?

Dry, it's generally no more slippery than polished tile or stone. Wet, it can be. A slip-resistant guard and bath mats help in kitchens and bathrooms.

Will my floor crack?

Polishing doesn't cause cracks, but existing ones will still show. They can be filled with color-matched filler. Concrete can also develop hairline cracks as a home settles.

Can I do polished concrete in a condo?

Ground-floor units often can. Upper floors usually need association approval and a sound-rated build-up. Microcement is often the better route.

Does polished concrete increase home value?

It can in modern and design-led homes. For resale, a neutral color and satin or semi-gloss finish have the widest appeal.

What if I find terrazzo under my carpet?

Consider restoring it instead. Original Miami terrazzo is valuable and often costs less to restore than a new floor.

Do I need a permit?

Polishing an existing slab generally doesn't need a building permit in Miami-Dade, but your contractor should confirm for your address. Condos and HOAs may need approval.

How long will it last?

With simple care, 20 years or more. It doesn't wear through like a coating does.
