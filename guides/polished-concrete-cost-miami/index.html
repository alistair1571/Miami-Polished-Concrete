Polished Concrete Cost in Miami (2026)

Most polished concrete in Miami costs $3 to $8 per square foot for a standard finish, and $8 to $15 for exposed aggregate or high gloss. A typical home with a satin finish lands around $6 to $9 per sq ft, or $6,000 to $9,000 for 1,000 sq ft, before any tile removal.

Cost by finish

Two choices drive most of the price: how deep the slab is ground (how much stone shows) and how far up the grit sequence it's polished (how much it shines). Each extra step is another pass with the machine.

By sheen

  • Matte / grind and seal: Final grit (approx.): 100–200; Typical Miami cost per sq ft: $3–$5; Where it's used: Warehouses, garages, back-of-house
  • Satin: Final grit (approx.): 400; Typical Miami cost per sq ft: $4–$7; Where it's used: Homes, offices, most retail
  • Semi-gloss: Final grit (approx.): 800; Typical Miami cost per sq ft: $5–$9; Where it's used: Homes, retail, restaurants
  • High gloss: Final grit (approx.): 1500–3000; Typical Miami cost per sq ft: $7–$12+; Where it's used: Showrooms, high-end retail, modern homes

By aggregate exposure

  • Cream: What's involved: Shallow grind, minimal stone; Effect on price: Cheapest
  • Salt and pepper: What's involved: A little deeper, fine specks show; Effect on price: Adds roughly $1 per sq ft
  • Exposed aggregate: What's involved: Deep grind to larger stone; Effect on price: Adds $2–$4 per sq ft; published Miami prices reach $8–$12+

Cost by project size

Setup, travel and edge work cost the same whether the floor is 200 or 2,000 sq ft, so small jobs cost more per foot.

  • Bathroom or small room (under 300 sq ft): Typical per sq ft: Top of the range, or a minimum charge; Why: Fixed setup costs; lots of edges
  • Single living area (500–1,500 sq ft): Typical per sq ft: $6–$9 (satin); Why: Most common residential job
  • Whole ground floor (1,500–3,000 sq ft): Typical per sq ft: $5–$8; Why: Setup spread over more floor
  • Commercial (5,000+ sq ft): Typical per sq ft: $3–$6; Why: Big machines, open areas, volume pricing

Room count matters too. An open-plan floor costs less than the same area split into small rooms, because every doorway, corner and wall edge is finished with hand tools.

Compare that with the floors it replaces: tile needs grout cleaning and resealing, VCT needs stripping and waxing, and coatings eventually need recoating.

How to keep the cost down

  • Choose satin rather than high gloss. Fewer passes, and it hides dust and footprints better.
  • Choose cream or salt and pepper rather than full exposed aggregate.
  • Do the whole floor at once so setup is spread over more square feet and you stay clear of minimum charges.
  • Plan it before cabinets and baseboards go in on a remodel or new build.
  • Ask about removal separately. A demo crew may be cheaper for tile removal. Make sure they contain the dust and don't gouge the slab.
  • Skip color unless you really want it; natural limestone-speckled concrete already reads warm. Staining options

How to read a polished concrete quote

Quotes vary threefold because "polished concrete" covers everything from a one-pass grind-and-seal to a multi-step high gloss. Check each quote states:

  • Grind depth: cream, salt and pepper or exposed aggregate. If it's not stated, ask.
  • Final grit or sheen: "polished" alone could mean anything from 200 to 3000 grit.
  • Densifier and guard: both should be named.
  • Prep work as separate lines: removal, repairs, joint filling.
  • A moisture test, essential if color or any coating is involved.
  • Asbestos results for pre-1984 adhesive.
  • Minimum charge, if the job is small.
  • Licence and insurance. You can verify Florida licences through the state's DBPR licence search.
  • Written warranty terms.

Red flag: a very low per-foot price that turns out to be a grind-and-seal with a topical sealer rather than a mechanically polished floor. Ask what grit they finish at.

Common questions

How much does polished concrete cost per square foot in Miami?

Published Miami prices mostly fall between $3 and $8 per sq ft for standard polishing, rising to $8 to $15 for exposed aggregate or high gloss. Most homes land around $6 to $9 for a satin finish.

How much does it cost to polish a 1,000 sq ft floor?

Typically $6,000 to $9,000 for a satin, salt-and-pepper finish in a home, before any tile removal. A plain matte finish on an open slab can come in lower.

Why is my small room so expensive per square foot?

Equipment setup, travel and edge work cost about the same for 200 sq ft as for 2,000 sq ft, and most contractors have a minimum charge. Doing several rooms at once brings the per-foot price down.

Is polished concrete cheaper than tile?

On an existing slab it's usually similar or cheaper to install, and cheaper to maintain because there's no grout to clean or reseal.

How much does maintenance cost?

Day to day, just a dust mop and pH-neutral cleaner. Every few years, traffic paths may need a professional burnish, which is far cheaper than re-polishing. Full re-polishing is only needed if the floor has been neglected or damaged.

Does the price include removing my old flooring?

Usually not. Tile removal typically adds $2 to $7 per sq ft and carpet $1 to $5, and some contractors subcontract it. Check that your quote says who removes it and how dust is contained.

Can I polish a floor that isn't concrete?

Not directly. Wood subfloors, such as upper floors of some houses, need waterproofing and a cement underlayment first, which adds cost. Microcement or an overlay may be the better option.
