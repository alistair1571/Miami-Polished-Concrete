Asbestos Under Old Floors in Miami Homes

If your home was built or renovated before the mid-1980s, the vinyl tiles and black adhesive on your slab may contain asbestos. That matters most if you're planning polished concrete, microcement or terrazzo restoration, because the first step of all three is grinding.

This guide explains which floors are suspect, why grinding is the worst thing you can do to them, how to test, what Florida and Miami-Dade rules say, and your options if the result is positive.

Which floors are suspect?

Asbestos was added to floor tiles and adhesives for strength, heat resistance and durability. Most manufacturers had phased it out of floor products by the late 1980s. In Miami homes, the usual suspects are:

  • 9-by-9-inch vinyl tiles (common 1950s–1970s): High: presume asbestos until tested
  • 12-by-12-inch vinyl tiles from the same era: Possible: many product lines contained it
  • Black or dark-brown "cutback" adhesive (mastic): High: can be positive even when the tile isn't
  • Sheet vinyl or linoleum with a backing: Possible: the backing may contain it
  • Ceramic tile on thinset: Usually low, but test old adhesive if it's dark
  • Carpet over bare slab or terrazzo: Low for the floor itself
  • Homes built after the late 1980s: Low

You can't tell by looking. Only a lab test confirms it.

Why grinding is the worst case

Intact vinyl tile and mastic are classed as nonfriable: the asbestos is locked into the material. They're relatively low-risk while they stay intact.

Grinding changes that. A diamond grinder turns tile and adhesive into fine powder, the friable state in which asbestos fibers become airborne and can be breathed in. Asbestos-related diseases can take 15 to 40 years to appear.

  • Florida's exemption for removing asbestos resilient flooring without an asbestos licence applies only while the material stays Category I nonfriable, and only when the work follows OSHA's asbestos standards.
  • Grinding, sanding or scraping it into dust takes it out of that category.
  • That's why the order matters: test, then remove intact, then grind and polish. Never the other way round.

How to test

  • Lab test of a sample: What it involves: A small piece of tile and a separate piece of adhesive sent to an NVLAP-accredited lab for PLM analysis; Cost: About $25–$50 per sample; 3–10 business days; Best for: A single room or a small area
  • Certified inspector: What it involves: Inspector collects samples, sends them to a lab and writes a report; Cost: About $200–$500 for a home; Best for: Several flooring types, larger projects, or if you'd rather not sample
  • Licensed asbestos consultant survey: What it involves: Required survey for regulated projects; Cost: Quote; Best for: Condo buildings over four units, commercial buildings
  • Test the tile and the adhesive separately. One can be positive and the other negative.
  • Sample each different floor in the house; different rooms were often done at different times.
  • If sampling yourself, follow the lab's instructions: wet the area, wear a disposable respirator and gloves, and seal the sample. If you're unsure, use an inspector.

Florida and Miami-Dade rules

This is a summary, not legal advice. Rules depend on the building and the work, and they change. Confirm with Miami-Dade DERM and the Florida DEP for your project.

  • Single-family home or building with 4 or fewer units: Florida's residential exemption generally removes the state survey and notification requirements
  • Condo or apartment building with more than 4 units: Not exempt: asbestos survey by a Florida-licensed consultant; Notice of Demolition or Asbestos Renovation to Miami-Dade DERM at least 10 working days before work
  • Commercial, mixed-use or public buildings: Not exempt: survey and DERM notification as above
  • Removing asbestos resilient flooring: No asbestos licence needed if it stays nonfriable, work meets OSHA standards, and written notice goes to DBPR; otherwise a licensed asbestos contractor
  • Anyone working for pay: OSHA's construction asbestos standard (29 CFR 1926.1101) protects workers

Even where exemptions apply, lenders, insurers, associations and local officials may still expect a survey. Condo owners should also check with their association.

If the test is positive: your options

  • Remove it properly, then finish the slab: What it means: Qualified contractor removes tile and mastic intact, with containment; then grinding and polishing can start; Works with polished concrete?: Yes: the only route to polishing
  • Leave it and cover it: What it means: Intact tiles stay in place under new flooring or a poured underlayment; Works with polished concrete?: No: the slab stays covered
  • Leave it alone for now: What it means: Intact, undamaged tile is low-risk if undisturbed; Works with polished concrete?: No

If you want polished concrete or terrazzo restoration, removal comes first. If you'd rather avoid removal, covering is an option, but with a different finished floor. Microcement and other overlays need a sound, well-bonded base, so ask an installer and an asbestos professional before putting anything over old vinyl.

What a good flooring contractor should do

  • Ask when the home was built and what's on the floor before quoting. Our quote form asks for the year for this reason.
  • Refuse to grind suspect tile or adhesive until it's been tested.
  • Explain the sequence: test, remove, then polish.
  • Know who to refer you to for testing and abatement.

Red flag: a contractor who says black mastic "just grinds off" without a test. Find another one.

Common questions

Do old floor tiles in Miami homes contain asbestos?

Many do. Vinyl floor tiles, especially 9-by-9-inch tiles from the 1950s through the 1970s, and the black adhesive under them often contain asbestos. Homes built or renovated before the mid-1980s should treat them as suspect until tested.

Can I grind asbestos mastic to polish my concrete?

No. Grinding turns intact tile and mastic into fine dust, which is the most dangerous way to disturb asbestos. Test first; if it's positive, the tile and mastic are removed by a qualified contractor using methods that keep it intact before any grinding starts.

How much does asbestos testing cost?

Lab analysis typically costs about $25 to $50 per sample, with results in 3 to 10 business days. A certified inspector usually charges $200 to $500 for a home assessment. Test the tile and the adhesive separately.

How much does asbestos floor tile removal cost?

Published 2026 figures mostly run $5 to $15 per sq ft, with most residential projects around $8 to $12. Mastic removal usually adds $2 to $5 per sq ft, and small rooms cost more per foot.

Do I need to notify Miami-Dade before removing asbestos flooring?

For a single-family home or a building with four or fewer units, Florida's residential exemption generally removes the survey and notification requirements. Condo buildings with more than four units and commercial buildings typically need a survey by a licensed consultant and notice to Miami-Dade DERM at least 10 working days before work. Confirm with DERM for your project.

Does the tile test clean mean I'm safe?

Not necessarily. The tile and the black adhesive under it are different materials; the tile can test negative while the mastic tests positive. Test both.

Can I just cover the old tiles instead?

You can leave intact tiles in place and cover them with new flooring or a poured underlayment, which avoids abatement. But you can't polish the concrete underneath without removing them first.

I think I was exposed. What should I do?

Stop the work, keep the area closed off, and don't sweep or vacuum dust with an ordinary vacuum. Talk to your doctor about the exposure, and have a licensed professional assess and clean the area.
