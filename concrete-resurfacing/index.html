Can my concrete be resurfaced?

Usually yes, if the slab is structurally sound: stains, surface flaking, hairline cracks, old coatings and a dated look are all fixable. Resurfacing won't fix sunken or lifted slabs, wide or moving cracks, tree-root damage or drainage problems. Those need repair, lifting or replacement first.

Avoid plain concrete paint on driveways and patios in Miami. It peels under hot tires and afternoon rain and makes the next resurfacing harder.

Concrete resurfacing by area

Driveways

The most common Miami resurfacing job. Choose a vehicle-rated overlay and sealer, fix rust stains at the source, and re-cut control joints through the overlay or it will crack along them. Work on the apron in the public right-of-way (between the sidewalk and the street) may need a permit from your city or Miami-Dade County.

Patios, lanais and Florida rooms

Spray knockdown is the go-to for uncovered patios: cool, textured and forgiving. Covered lanais can go smooth or stamped. Keep the overlay below sliding door tracks and thresholds so rainwater can't flow inside.

Walkways and front entries

Stamped or stenciled patterns, borders and color bands work well. Add slip resistance, because front steps get wet.

Pool decks

Pool decks have their own challenges: heat, chlorine and salt, and the coping joint. See pool deck resurfacing.

Interior floors

Damaged, patched or uneven interior slabs can be resurfaced with a self-leveling or polished overlay, or microcement, then finished. Ideal after a tile tear-out leaves a rough slab.

Commercial and condo flatwork

Storefront walkways, restaurant patios, condo walkways and entrances. Accessibility standards limit changes in level along accessible routes, so resurfacing should remove lips and ridges, not create them. Walkways and decks with structural concrete damage need an engineer before any resurfacing.

Common Miami concrete problems, and whether resurfacing fixes them

  • Orange rust stains. Very common in Miami-Dade, usually from irrigation fed by iron-rich well water. Resurfacing covers them, but they'll come back unless you redirect sprinklers or treat the water.
  • Black mildew and algae. Shaded, damp slabs grow mildew fast. Clean and treat before resurfacing, and use a mildew-resistant sealer.
  • Tree roots. Ficus, banyan and other big-root trees lift slabs. Resurfacing a lifted slab doesn't work; the damaged sections need replacing.
  • Heat. Dark overlays get very hot in the sun. Choose light colors and textured finishes for barefoot areas.
  • Rain and slip. Afternoon storms make smooth or glossy surfaces slippery. Choose texture or add anti-slip grit.
  • White haze (efflorescence). Salts carried up by moisture leave white deposits. They're cleaned off first, and breathable sealers help.
  • Sealer turning white. Moisture trapped under solvent sealers is common here. See sealers that suit Florida.
  • Pavers vs resurfacing. Resurfacing is usually cheaper and seamless. Pavers are easier to repair section by section. Overlays shouldn't go over pavers, because pavers move independently.

How concrete resurfacing works

  1. Assessment: soundness, cracks, drainage and moisture, with photos and measurements.
  2. Surface prep: pressure wash, remove stains, mildew and old sealers, then grind or shot blast. Prep decides whether the overlay bonds.
  3. Repairs: cracks routed and filled, spalls patched, control joints marked.
  4. Prime with a bonding primer.
  5. Base coat: polymer-modified cement smooths the surface.
  6. Finish coat: sprayed and knocked down, troweled, stamped or stenciled, with color.
  7. Joints re-cut through the overlay.
  8. Seal with a UV-stable, breathable sealer, plus anti-slip grit outdoors.

How long does it last?

A professionally installed polymer overlay typically lasts 10 to 15 years or more on a sound slab, with resealing every 2 to 3 years outdoors. Resurfacing doesn't add structural strength; it protects and renews the surface, and cracks that move in the slab below will usually show through over time.

Maintaining resurfaced concrete

  • Sweep and rinse regularly. Pressure wash gently on a wide fan tip.
  • Reseal outdoor surfaces every 2 to 3 years, more often on driveways and sunny patios.
  • Adjust sprinklers so they don't soak the surface, especially with well water.
  • Clean oil and rust promptly.

Common questions

What is concrete resurfacing?

Applying a new polymer-modified cement layer over existing concrete to renew its surface, color and texture without tearing it out.

Can cracked concrete be resurfaced?

Hairline and small cracks, yes, after they're repaired. Wide or moving cracks, sunken slabs and root damage need fixing or replacement first, or they'll come back through the overlay.

How much does it cost to resurface a driveway in Miami?

A two-car driveway of about 600 sq ft typically runs about $1,800 to $4,800 for a basic spray texture or resurfacer, and more for stamped overlays. Repairs and prep add cost.

How long does resurfaced concrete last?

Usually 10 to 15 years or more on a sound slab, with resealing every 2 to 3 years outdoors.

Is resurfacing cheaper than replacing concrete?

Usually much cheaper, with no demolition, disposal or re-pour, as long as the slab underneath is sound.

How long before I can drive on it?

Typically 3 to 7 days. Foot traffic is usually fine after about 24 hours.

Will resurfacing fix rust stains?

It covers them, but they'll return if the source (usually iron-rich well water from sprinklers) isn't fixed.

Does resurfacing make concrete stronger?

No. It renews and protects the surface. Structural problems must be fixed separately.

What's the best resurfacing for a Miami patio?

Spray knockdown in a light color. It's textured, slip resistant and stays cooler barefoot.

Can you resurface over pavers?

It's not recommended, because pavers move independently and the overlay will crack. Reset, replace or seal pavers instead.

Do I need a permit?

Usually not for resurfacing private flatwork, but work on the driveway apron in the public right-of-way may need one, and HOAs may need to approve colors. Your contractor should confirm.

Can I resurface concrete myself?

DIY resurfacers exist, but prep, primers, joint cutting and weather timing are where most jobs fail. Miami's heat and rain make DIY harder.
