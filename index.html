Why Miami is switching to polished concrete

Most Miami homes and nearly every commercial building sit on a concrete slab. Under the tile, carpet or laminate there is already a floor that can be ground, densified and polished into a finish that lasts for decades. That's why polished concrete has become the default for Wynwood galleries, Doral warehouses, Brickell lofts and more and more family homes in Kendall and Pinecrest.

It also suits the climate better than almost anything else. It stays cool underfoot, it doesn't swell or cup in humidity like wood and laminate, it gives mold nowhere to hide, and after a flood it can be cleaned and dried rather than ripped out. For a city that lives with heat, sand, salt air and hurricane season, that matters more than it does in most places.

What is concrete polishing?

Heavy grinders fitted with diamond tooling cut down the top of the slab, a densifier hardens it, and progressively finer grits refine it until it's smooth, dense and reflective. No wax or coating sits on top. The shine is the concrete itself. See how concrete polishing works.

Choosing your look

Two choices shape the floor: how much stone shows (smooth cream, salt and pepper, medium or full aggregate) and how much it shines (matte, satin, semi-gloss or high gloss). Satin salt and pepper is the most popular choice for Miami homes. Compare finishes.

Polished concrete for Miami homes

Polished concrete is one of the lowest-maintenance floors you can put in a home. There are no grout lines to scrub, no planks to cup in the humidity and nothing for sand, dog claws or kids' toys to chip. A dust mop and a pH-neutral cleaner are usually all it needs.

The honest limits: concrete is hard underfoot, it will show hairline cracks that were already in the slab, and acidic spills like lime juice, wine and vinegar can etch the surface if they sit. A good stain guard and rugs in the right places handle most of this. See residential polished concrete.

Condos and apartments

Most Florida condo associations require written approval and a sound-rated underlayment before a hard floor goes in, and high-rise slabs are often post-tensioned, which limits how deep a contractor can safely grind. In many condos, microcement over a sound-rated build-up is the practical way to get the look. See microcement for condos.

Found terrazzo under the carpet?

Thousands of 1950s and 60s homes in Miami, Miami Beach, Coral Gables and North Miami have original terrazzo hiding under carpet or tile. It can often be restored for less than a new floor. See terrazzo restoration.

Commercial polished concrete in Miami

Retail stores, warehouses, offices, car dealerships and parking garages are choosing polished concrete because it stands up to heavy foot traffic, pallet jacks and forklifts without peeling, and it never needs waxing or stripping. An old, dusty warehouse slab can be converted in days.

  • Joint filling with semi-rigid polyurea to protect edges from hard wheels
  • Dust-proofing densifiers on floors that shed concrete dust onto stock
  • Phased, overnight or weekend work so the business stays open
  • Line striping and zone markings

Epoxy, garages and pool decks

Where you need chemical resistance, bright colors or a fully sealed surface, a resin floor is the better choice. In Miami, the most common reason epoxy fails is moisture coming up through the slab, so a moisture test comes first. Epoxy flooring and garage floors explain the options. Outside, pool decks and patios need textured, cool finishes rather than polish. See pool deck resurfacing and concrete resurfacing.

Benefits of polished concrete

  • Low maintenance. No wax, no stripping, no recoating. It doesn't hold dust, dirt or allergens.
  • Long life. A properly polished and maintained floor can last 20 years or more.
  • Cost-effective. You're finishing a slab you already have instead of buying a new floor to put on top of it.
  • Design range. Aggregate, sheen, color, patterns and inlays.
  • Brighter spaces. Reflective floors bounce light and can reduce lighting needs in commercial buildings.
  • Seamless and hygienic. No grout lines for bacteria and mold to collect in.
  • Built for Miami. Cool underfoot, unaffected by humidity, and far more forgiving of flooding than wood, laminate or carpet.

Is polished concrete slippery?

Not when it's done right. Dry, polished concrete is typically as slip-resistant as many polished stones and glazed tiles. Wet, it can become slippery like any smooth hard floor. For commercial spaces, ask the contractor to test to the ANSI A326.3 standard (a wet DCOF of 0.42 or higher is the benchmark for level interior floors that may get wet) and to use an anti-slip guard where needed.

How to choose a concrete polishing contractor

Ask any contractor, including ours, these questions before signing:

  1. Is this true polish or grind and seal?
  2. What grit will you finish at, and what aggregate exposure should I expect?
  3. Will you test the slab for moisture, and how?
  4. What happens to cracks and joints?
  5. Can you show me photos of Miami floors, and a sample or test patch on mine?
  6. Can I see your license and proof of liability and workers' comp insurance?
  7. For condos: will you handle the association paperwork?
  8. What's the warranty, and what does it exclude?

Common questions

How long does polished concrete last?

With basic care, 20 years or more in a home. The densified surface doesn't wear through like a coating does. Busy commercial floors may need re-burnishing or a light re-polish every few years to restore the gloss.

How long does installation take?

Most Miami homes take 2 to 4 days once old flooring is removed. You can usually walk on the floor as soon as it's done. Large commercial floors are phased around your business.

Can any concrete floor be polished?

Most can. Very soft, badly cracked, heavily patched or moisture-affected slabs may be better suited to a polished overlay, microcement or a coating. A site visit and moisture test will tell you.

Can you polish concrete after removing tile?

Yes, and it's one of the most common jobs in Miami. Expect some marks from old thinset and patterns from the tile layout. Many owners like the character; if not, a deeper grind, dye or overlay can even it out.

Is polished concrete good for Miami's humidity?

It's one of the best floors for it. Concrete doesn't swell or warp, and polished concrete breathes, so it isn't prone to the bubbling that sealed coatings can suffer on damp slabs.

Does polished concrete stain?

It's stain resistant, not stain proof. Oils, red wine, coffee, lime juice and vinegar should be wiped up quickly. Acidic spills can dull the surface if they sit. A stain guard helps, and dull spots can be re-polished.

Is polished concrete cold or hard?

It's cool underfoot, which most Miami owners count as a plus. It is hard, so rugs are a good idea where you stand for long periods, like in front of the kitchen sink.

Polished concrete or epoxy?

Polished concrete has no coating to peel, lasts longer and needs less upkeep. Epoxy wins where you need chemical resistance, bright colors or a fully sealed surface, such as labs, workshops and some garages.

Do I need a permit?

Polishing an existing slab generally doesn't need a building permit. Condos usually need written association approval. Structural repairs or new pours may need permits. Your contractor should confirm for your address.

Do I need to move out?

Usually not for the whole house, but you'll need to stay out of the rooms being worked on. Grinding is noisy, and good contractors use dust extraction to keep dust down.

Is there asbestos risk?

In homes built before the mid-1980s, old black adhesive under tile or carpet can contain asbestos. Get it tested before grinding. Our form asks for the year your home was built so the contractor can flag this.

How do I clean polished concrete?

Dust mop daily or as needed, and damp mop with a pH-neutral cleaner. Avoid vinegar, bleach, citrus cleaners and wax. See maintenance.
