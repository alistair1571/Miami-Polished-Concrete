What is polished concrete?

Polished concrete is an existing or new concrete slab ground with diamond tooling, hardened with a chemical densifier, and refined through finer and finer grits until it's smooth and reflective. It's a bit like sanding wood, except the machines weigh several hundred pounds and the abrasives are industrial diamonds.

The shine comes from the concrete itself, not from anything sitting on top. That's the difference that matters. A coating wears off, scratches through and needs replacing. A polished floor wears slowly and can be re-polished rather than stripped and redone.

It's not the same as a sealed floor

A lot of floors sold as "polished" in Miami are actually ground and then coated with a glossy sealer. They look similar on day one, but the sealer wears in traffic lanes and needs recoating every one to three years. Ask what grit the floor will be finished at and whether a topical sealer is part of the job.

Commercial and institutional

  • Retail stores and showrooms, including car dealerships and furniture stores
  • Warehouses, distribution and packing facilities in Doral, Medley and Hialeah
  • Hotel lobbies, restaurants (front of house) and bars
  • Offices, co-working spaces and studios

  • Galleries and event spaces in Wynwood and the Design District

Residential

  • Living areas and open-plan kitchens after a tile tear-out
  • Whole-home remodels and new builds
  • Garages
  • Covered patios and Florida rooms
  • Additions and ADUs built on a new slab

Where it's not the right choice

  • Commercial kitchens: use urethane cement, which handles hot water, grease and thermal shock. See epoxy flooring.
  • Chemical areas and labs: a sealed epoxy system is better.
  • Upper-floor condos: association sound rules and post-tensioned slabs usually point to microcement.
  • Uncovered pool decks and driveways: a polished surface is too slippery when wet outdoors. See pool deck resurfacing.

How concrete polishing works, step by step

  1. Hardness test. The contractor tests the slab's surface hardness (usually on the Mohs scale) to choose the right diamond tooling. Soft slabs need hard-bond diamonds and vice versa. Get this wrong and the floor glazes or scratches.
  2. Moisture and condition check. In-slab humidity testing (ASTM F2170), crack mapping and a look at old adhesives. On homes built before the mid-1980s, black mastic is tested for asbestos before any grinding. See the asbestos guide.
  3. Surface prep. Old tile, thinset, glue, paint or coatings come off. Holes, cracks and low spots are noted.
  4. Grinding. Metal-bond diamonds (roughly 30 to 150 grit) flatten the surface and set the aggregate exposure. Each pass removes the scratches of the one before.
  5. Repairs and grout coat. Cracks and joints are filled with epoxy or polyurea. A thin grout coat or pore filler seals pinholes so the finish looks clean.
  6. Densifying. A lithium, sodium or potassium silicate hardener soaks in and reacts with the concrete to make the surface harder and dust-free.
  7. Polishing. Resin-bond diamonds go up in grit, typically 100, 200, 400 and 800, then 1500 or 3000 for high gloss. Dye goes in here if you've chosen color.
  8. Guard and burnish. A penetrating stain guard goes on and is burnished in with a high-speed machine to lock in the sheen.

Wet or dry polishing?

Most interior Miami jobs are polished dry, with HEPA vacuum extraction pulling dust straight off the grinder. It's faster and there's no slurry to clean up. Wet polishing uses water to cool the tooling and control dust. It's sometimes used on very hard slabs or outdoors, but it creates slurry that has to be collected and disposed of properly.

Planning polished concrete on a new slab

Building a new home, addition or commercial space? The best polished floors are planned before the pour. Tell your general contractor early, because the decisions they make on pour day decide how the floor looks.

  • Mix: a consistent mix and aggregate, ideally one supplier and one batch plan
  • Finishing: hard-troweled, flat and level. Avoid burn marks from over-troweling.
  • No curing compounds or membranes that block the densifier, unless they're compatible with polishing
  • Protect the slab during construction. Oil, drywall mud, paint and tape all leave marks that show up in the polish.
  • Control joints: plan the layout with the design, because they'll be visible
  • Cure time: the slab usually cures for around 28 days before polishing

What your polished floor will really look like

Polished concrete is a natural material, and no two slabs finish the same. Set these expectations up front and you'll love the result:

  • Color variation and mottling are normal, especially on existing slabs.
  • Hairline cracks already in the slab will still show. They can be filled but not erased. New cracks can appear as a building settles.
  • Old tile layouts, patches and thinset marks may leave "ghost" patterns after a tile tear-out. Many owners like the character; a darker dye or deeper grind reduces them.
  • Control joints stay visible and are part of the look.
  • Etching: acidic spills like lime juice, wine, soda and vinegar can dull the surface if left. A guard slows this; a re-polish fixes it.

That's why a test patch matters. Seeing a square of your own floor at your chosen finish removes the guesswork.

Polished concrete pros and cons

Pros

  • Very long life, 20+ years in most homes with basic care
  • No wax, stripping or recoating
  • Doesn't peel, chip or delaminate like a coating
  • Won't swell, warp or cup in humidity
  • Survives flooding far better than wood, laminate or carpet
  • Seamless, with no grout to hold dirt, mold or allergens
  • Cool underfoot in the Miami heat
  • Reflects light, which cuts lighting needs in commercial spaces
  • Uses the slab you already have, with less waste. Can contribute to LEED points.

Cons

  • Hard underfoot, and dropped glasses break
  • Existing cracks and marks remain visible
  • Acidic spills can etch the surface if left
  • Can be slippery when wet without the right guard
  • Noisier acoustically than carpet, so rugs help in large rooms
  • Not suited to every slab. Soft, damaged or damp slabs may need an overlay or a different floor.

Cost over time

A polished floor usually costs more to install than grind and seal or a basic coating, but it doesn't need recoating. Over 10 to 15 years, it is often the cheapest floor in total cost of ownership, especially in commercial spaces where waxing and stripping VCT or recoating epoxy adds up.

Caring for a polished concrete floor

  • Dust mop regularly. Sand is the main thing that dulls Miami floors.
  • Damp mop with a pH-neutral cleaner.
  • Never use vinegar, bleach, citrus or ammonia cleaners, or wax.
  • Wipe spills quickly, especially lime, wine, coffee and oils.
  • Use walk-off mats at entrances and felt pads under furniture.
  • Commercial floors benefit from periodic burnishing. Homes may want a light re-polish or fresh guard every few years.

Common questions

Can my existing floor be polished?

Most sound slabs can. The contractor checks hardness, moisture, cracks and old adhesive. Soft, heavily damaged or damp slabs may be better suited to a polished overlay or microcement.

Can you polish concrete after removing tile?

Yes, it's one of the most common Miami jobs. Thinset is ground off, but faint patterns from the old tile layout may show. A test patch will show what to expect.

Is polished concrete slippery?

Dry, it's usually as grippy as or grippier than polished stone and glazed tile. Wet, it can get slippery like any smooth hard floor. Commercial floors should meet the ANSI A326.3 wet DCOF benchmark of 0.42 for level interior floors, and an anti-slip guard can help.

Does polished concrete crack?

Polishing doesn't cause cracks, but it reveals the ones already in the slab. Concrete can also crack as buildings settle. Cracks can be filled with color-matched epoxy or polyurea.

How long does polished concrete last?

The polish itself lasts 20 years or more in most homes. Busy commercial floors may need burnishing or a light re-polish every few years.

Is polished concrete good for Miami's climate?

It's one of the best choices. It doesn't react to humidity, stays cool, gives mold nowhere to grow and can be cleaned and dried after water damage.

Is it dusty and noisy to install?

Grinding is loud. Good contractors use HEPA vacuum extraction attached to the grinders, so dust is minimal. You'll need to stay out of the rooms being worked on.

Can polished concrete be colored?

Yes. It can be dyed or acid stained on an existing slab, or colored integrally in new concrete.

Does it need sealing?

Not in the way coatings do. It's densified and finished with a penetrating guard, which may be refreshed every few years.

Can you polish concrete outdoors?

Covered patios and lanais, yes. Uncovered pool decks and driveways are too slippery when wet, so textured resurfacing is the better choice.

Will I see a sample first?

You should. Ask for a test patch on your own slab at the finish you want before signing.
