What's the best epoxy floor for my space?

  • Commercial kitchens, breweries, food production: urethane cement
  • Labs, clinics, restrooms, locker rooms: quartz or flake broadcast with an integral cove base
  • Warehouses and manufacturing: high-build epoxy with a polyurethane topcoat, or polished concrete if you don't need a coating
  • Electronics, data rooms, pharma: static-dissipative or conductive ESD epoxy
  • Showrooms and homes: metallic or solid-color epoxy with a UV-stable topcoat
  • Garages: flake with a polyaspartic topcoat (see garage floors)
  • Fast turnaround: polyaspartic, or MMA for the fastest return to service

Epoxy flooring by industry

Restaurants and commercial kitchens

Epoxy alone often fails in kitchens. Hot water, steam cleaning, grease and thermal shock break it down. Urethane cement is the standard kitchen floor, with an integral cove base up the wall, correct slope to drains, a textured finish for wet slip resistance, and a system that tolerates ground-floor slab moisture. Florida restaurant inspections look for floors that are smooth, durable, non-absorbent and easily cleanable.

Food and beverage production, breweries, distilleries

Urethane cement handles wash-downs, sugars, acids and hot water. Plan the floor with the drainage, curbs and cleaning chemicals you actually use. There's no single certificate that makes a floor compliant: materials may be USDA-accepted or FDA-compliant, but compliance depends on the installed system, cove base, drainage and cleanability.

Healthcare, veterinary, labs and pharma

Seamless floors with integral cove base are easier to disinfect than tile or sheet vinyl. Confirm the system resists the disinfectants you use.

Electronics, data centers and clean manufacturing (ESD)

Static-dissipative floors bleed charge off slowly; conductive floors move it quickly to ground. Floors should be tested to ANSI/ESD standards after installation, and footwear and grounding are part of the system.

Warehouses, battery rooms and hangars

Choose epoxy when you need chemical resistance, bright colors or marked zones, and polished concrete when you just need a durable, dust-free floor. Battery acid attacks standard epoxy, so charging areas need a novolac or vinyl ester system. Hangars and fleet shops near Opa-locka, MIA and Homestead need fuel- and hydraulic-fluid-resistant systems in light, reflective colors.

Cold storage and freezers

Standard epoxy becomes brittle in freezers. Urethane cement or MMA systems rated for low temperatures are used, and MMA can go down with the freezer still cold.

Retail, showrooms, salons, gyms and schools

Metallic, flake and solid-color systems give a branded, seamless look. Flake or quartz suits restrooms and cafeterias; epoxy terrazzo suits lobbies. Use rubber in free-weight zones.

Condo and hotel parking decks

Since Florida introduced milestone structural inspections for condo buildings, many Miami associations have needed concrete repairs on parking decks and walkways. Once structural repairs are done under an engineer's direction, decks, ramps and balconies are usually protected with a traffic coating: a waterproofing membrane built for vehicles and pedestrians that keeps water and chlorides out of the concrete and rebar.

Traffic coatings are a specialist, engineer-specified system, not a standard epoxy floor. If your association is planning deck work, tell us in the form and we'll route the request accordingly.

Metallic and epoxy floors inside your home

  • It scratches. Grit, dog claws and dragged chairs mark glossy epoxy. A tough polyurethane topcoat helps, and satin finishes hide scratches better.
  • UV. Epoxy yellows near big windows and sliding doors, so a UV-stable topcoat is essential in bright Miami homes.
  • Moisture. Ground-floor slabs must be tested. It's the main reason residential epoxy fails here.
  • It's a coating. It will eventually need a fresh topcoat in busy areas.
  • Condos: association approval and sound rules apply. See microcement for condos.

Why epoxy floors fail in Miami, and how to prevent it

  1. Moisture vapor from the slab. The number one cause. Slabs near Miami's high water table push moisture up, and it collects under the coating as bubbles and blisters. Prevent it: test the slab (in-slab RH to ASTM F2170, or moisture emission testing), then use a moisture-mitigating primer where needed.
  2. Humidity and dew point during install. High humidity can cause amine blush, a greasy film that stops the next coat bonding. Prevent it: check humidity and dew point before each coat.
  3. Outgassing. As a slab warms up, air escapes and leaves pinholes. Prevent it: coat when slab temperature is steady or falling, not in the heat of a Miami afternoon.
  4. Poor prep. Acid etching leaves weak concrete and contaminants. Prevent it: diamond grinding or shot blasting to the required profile.
  5. Wrong system for the use. Epoxy in kitchens, standard epoxy in battery rooms, thin coatings under forklifts. Prevent it: choose the resin for the exposure.
  6. UV. Epoxy yellows in sunlight. Prevent it: a UV-stable topcoat.

For GCs, architects and facility engineers

Resinous floors usually fall under CSI MasterFormat section 09 67 23 (Resinous Flooring). Specify each layer's product and thickness, the prep method and concrete surface profile, moisture testing method and limits plus the mitigation product, cove base details, joint treatment, slip resistance target, chemical resistance for the listed exposures, a mock-up, and a manufacturer-approved installer with a system warranty.

Warranties and care

Ask whether the warranty is installer-only or a manufacturer system warranty, and check the exclusions: moisture failures without mitigation, chemicals outside the stated list, impact damage, UV yellowing and slab cracking are common. Day to day, sweep daily, clean with a pH-neutral cleaner, rinse chemical spills promptly, and recoat topcoats in traffic lanes before they wear through.

Questions to ask

  1. Which resin system, and why for my exposures?
  2. How will you test for moisture, and what if it's high?
  3. How will you prep: grinding, shot blasting or etching?
  4. How thick is each layer?
  5. Is there a cove base, and how are drains and joints handled?
  6. Is the topcoat UV-stable and chemical-resistant?
  7. Are you a manufacturer-approved installer?

Common questions

How much does epoxy flooring cost in Miami?

Published Miami prices run about $5 to $15 per sq ft installed, depending on the system. Thin coatings cost least; urethane cement, ESD and epoxy terrazzo are priced after a site survey. Moisture mitigation and cove base add cost.

Why do epoxy floors peel in Miami?

Usually moisture vapor from the slab, poor prep or the wrong system for the use. Testing the slab and grinding properly prevent most failures.

What's the best floor for a commercial kitchen?

Urethane cement with an integral cove base. Standard epoxy struggles with hot water, grease and thermal shock.

Is epoxy flooring slippery?

Smooth gloss epoxy can be slippery when wet. Flake, quartz or grit additives in the topcoat give adjustable slip resistance.

How long does epoxy flooring last?

From a few years for thin coatings in heavy traffic to 15 to 20 years or more for thick commercial systems, with topcoat renewal.

How long does installation take?

Many commercial floors are done over a few days or a weekend. Fast-cure systems like polyaspartic and MMA can reopen within hours.

Can epoxy go over tile?

Sometimes, with the right prep and primer, but it's often better to remove the tile. Grout lines can telegraph through thinner systems.

Is epoxy flooring food-safe?

Many systems use USDA-accepted or FDA-compliant materials, but food safety depends on the whole installation: seamless, cove base, drainage and cleanability.

What's the difference between static-dissipative and conductive flooring?

Both control static. Conductive floors move charge to ground faster. Which you need depends on your equipment and ESD program. Floors should be tested after install.

Does epoxy yellow?

Epoxy can yellow in UV light. UV-stable polyurethane or polyaspartic topcoats prevent it.

Can epoxy be used outdoors?

Standard epoxy isn't recommended outdoors in Miami because of UV and moisture. Use UV-stable systems, or textured pool deck and patio finishes.

Epoxy or polished concrete for a warehouse?

Polished concrete for dry storage and forklift traffic. Epoxy for chemicals, battery charging, clean areas or color-coded zones.
